# Real Estate Prospecting — Objection Handling Scripts

**Method**: Feel → Felt → Found + Bridge to Value  
**Tone**: Conversational, empathetic, never pushy. The goal is to keep the door open, not "win" the argument.

---

## 1. "I'm not interested"

> **Prospect**: I'm not interested, thanks.  
> **You**: Totally fair — and honestly, most people aren't until the timing's right. I'm not calling to sell you anything today. I just wanted to introduce myself since I work with a lot of homeowners in [neighborhood]. Quick question though — if you *were* to consider selling in the next year or two, do you have a ballpark idea of what your home's worth right now?

**Why it works**: Reframes from "sales call" to "resource." The question is low-pressure but opens a conversation about value — and everyone's curious about their home's value.

**If they still say no**: "No problem at all. If anything changes, I'm Chris Hoover with The Hoover Home Team — easy to find. Have a great day."

---

## 2. "I already have an agent / I know someone"

> **Prospect**: I already work with someone.  
> **You**: That's great — having a good agent relationship matters. I'm not trying to step on anyone's toes. Out of curiosity, are they keeping you updated on what's happening in [neighborhood/market]? Because things have shifted a lot lately — [insert one specific market stat, e.g., "inventory in Cape Coral is down 18% year over year"].

**Why it works**: Doesn't challenge their loyalty. Instead, plants a seed — is their current agent actually providing value? The market stat demonstrates *your* value without being pushy.

**If they're firm**: "Sounds like you're in good hands. If you ever want a second opinion on pricing or the market, my door's always open."

---

## 3. "I want to wait for the market to improve"

> **Prospect**: I'm going to wait until the market gets better.  
> **You**: I hear that a lot, and it makes sense on the surface. Can I share what I'm actually seeing? In [market], prices are [current trend], but here's the thing — buyer demand is actually [up/stable] and inventory is [low/dropping]. What that means is, homes priced right are still moving. The risk of waiting is that if rates drop, you'll suddenly have *more* competition from other sellers. Would it be worth a quick 15-minute market review so you can see the actual numbers for your specific home?

**Why it works**: Acknowledges their concern, then reframes with data. Doesn't argue — educates. The "risk of waiting" angle is compelling without being fear-based.

---

## 4. "I can sell it myself (FSBO)"

> **Prospect**: I'm going to try selling it on my own first.  
> **You**: Respect that — you know your home better than anyone. A lot of sellers start there. The thing I see with FSBOs in [area] is that they typically sell for about 6-10% less than agent-listed homes, even after you factor in commission. It's usually not about marketing — it's about negotiation leverage and buyer qualification. Here's what I'd offer: let me run a quick comp analysis for you, no strings. That way you at least know exactly what your home should sell for, whether you use me or not.

**Why it works**: Validates their choice, shares a compelling stat, then offers *free value*. The comp analysis gets you in the door and demonstrates competence.

---

## 5. "Your commission is too high"

> **Prospect**: I don't want to pay that much in commission.  
> **You**: I completely get it — that's real money. Let me ask you this: if I can show you how my marketing and negotiation strategy nets you *more* after commission than you'd get with a discount agent or on your own, would that change the conversation? Because that's exactly what happens most of the time. My last [X] listings sold for an average of [X]% above asking. The commission isn't a cost — it's an investment with a measurable return.

**Why it works**: Shifts the frame from "cost" to "net outcome." Never apologize for commission or offer to discount — compete on value.

---

## 6. "Just send me some information"

> **Prospect**: Can you just send me some info?  
> **You**: Absolutely — I'll send that over today. So I send you the *right* info, quick question: are you more curious about what your home is worth right now, or about what the market's doing in your area? ... Great. And just so I'm not sending you generic stuff — what's your email, and is [address] still your current home?

**Why it works**: "Send me info" is usually a brush-off. This script turns it into a qualifying conversation. You get their email, confirm the property, and learn their motivation — all while they think they're controlling the interaction.

---

## 7. "I need to talk to my spouse"

> **Prospect**: I need to talk to my wife/husband about it.  
> **You**: Of course — that's a big decision and you should absolutely be on the same page. Would it help if I put together a quick one-page summary of what we talked about? That way you can share it with [spouse's name] and they'll have the same info you do. And then maybe we could all hop on a quick call together — even 10 minutes — so they can ask their own questions?

**Why it works**: Supports the decision process instead of fighting it. The one-pager creates a touchpoint, and suggesting a group call keeps momentum without being pushy.

---

## 8. "The Zestimate says my home is worth $X"

> **Prospect**: Zillow says my house is worth $450K, so I want to list at $460K.  
> **You**: Zillow's a great starting point — I actually use it as one of several data sources. The thing is, Zestimates have a median error rate of about 6-7% nationally, and it can be even wider in [market]. They can't account for your upgrades, your lot, or what's actually closing in your specific neighborhood. What I do is pull actual sold comps within the last 90 days, adjust for your home's specifics, and give you a price that'll attract the *right* buyers. Want me to run that for you? I bet we'll find the real number is different — could be higher, could be lower, but it'll be accurate.

**Why it works**: Doesn't trash Zillow (they'll get defensive). Educates on its limitations, positions your CMA as the accurate alternative.

---

## Quick Reference — Universal Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Never argue** | Acknowledge → Redirect → Offer value |
| **Ask, don't tell** | Questions keep them talking and engaged |
| **One specific stat** | Market data builds instant credibility |
| **Always offer something free** | CMA, market report, one-pager — get in the door |
| **Name drop the neighborhood** | Shows you're local, not a cold-call factory |
| **Set the next step** | Every conversation ends with a clear next action |
| **Know when to exit gracefully** | A good goodbye today = a callback tomorrow |
