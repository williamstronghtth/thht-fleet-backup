# FinCEN Real Estate Reporting Guide
## For Chris Hoover - Prepared March 6, 2026

---

## CRITICAL: Does This Apply to Suzanne?

**The FinCEN Real Estate Rule ONLY applies if:**
1. ✅ Residential real estate transfer
2. ✅ All-cash OR no institutional lender financing
3. ⚠️ **At least one buyer is an ENTITY (LLC, corporation, trust, etc.)**

**If Suzanne is buying as an INDIVIDUAL (her own name, not through an LLC or trust), she does NOT need to file the FinCEN report.** The title company may still require identity verification, but that's different from the FinCEN entity reporting.

---

## If She IS Buying Through an Entity/Trust

### What Information Must Be Reported:

**Property Information:**
- Street address
- Legal description  
- Date of closing

**For Entity Purchases (LLC, Corp, etc.):**
- Legal name of entity
- Current street address
- IRS Tax ID (EIN)
- EACH beneficial owner (anyone with 25%+ ownership OR substantial control)
- Each beneficial owner's: full legal name, DOB, residential address, citizenship, SSN

**For Trust Purchases:**
- Legal name of trust
- Date trust was executed
- IRS Tax ID
- Whether trust is revocable
- Trustee information
- Beneficiary information (if they can demand distributions)
- Grantor/settlor info (if trust is revocable)

**Seller Information:**
- Full legal name
- DOB
- Current residential address
- IRS TIN

**Financial Information:**
- Total consideration paid
- Each payment: amount, method, account number, originating bank, payor

---

## Exemptions (Transactions NOT Reportable)

**Transactional Exemptions:**
- Transfer due to death (will/trust)
- Divorce/dissolution
- Bankruptcy
- Court-ordered transfers

**If she's using financing from a bank/lender**, the transaction may not be reportable under FinCEN (it's targeting all-cash entity purchases for money laundering prevention).

---

## What The Title Company Likely Sent Her

The title company probably sent a packet including:
1. **Identity Verification Forms** - Standard on all transactions
2. **Source of Funds Documentation** - Where is the money coming from
3. **FinCEN Certification** - May ask her to certify she's not an entity OR to provide entity info if she is
4. **Wire Fraud Warnings** - Standard disclosures
5. **ALTA Settlement Statement** - The numbers breakdown

---

## Talking Points for Monday Call

1. **First, clarify HOW she's buying:**
   - Her name only? → FinCEN entity reporting likely doesn't apply
   - Through an LLC or trust? → She'll need to provide beneficial owner info

2. **Identity verification is NOT the same as FinCEN reporting**
   - Title companies verify identity on ALL transactions
   - FinCEN reporting is specifically for entity purchases

3. **Source of funds is standard**
   - They need to know where the money is coming from
   - Bank statements, retirement account statements, etc.
   - This prevents wire fraud and money laundering

4. **The forms look scary but are usually simple**
   - Most of the 50-page packet is disclosures and boilerplate
   - The actual forms she needs to complete are usually 2-3 pages

5. **Offer to do a 3-way call with the title company**
   - They can tell her exactly which pages need attention
   - Most title companies are happy to walk through it

## Bottom Line

If Suzanne is buying in her own name (not an LLC/trust) with a mortgage (not all-cash), the FinCEN entity reporting likely doesn't apply to her at all. The title company paperwork she's dealing with is probably standard identity verification and closing documents — annoying but not unusual.

The key is figuring out exactly which forms she actually needs to complete vs. which are just disclosures she needs to acknowledge.
