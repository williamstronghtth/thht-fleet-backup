# Engineering Management for the Rest of Us - Sarah Drasner
**For Engineers Who Became Managers (Without Wanting to Be "That Manager")**

## About the Author
- Sarah Drasner: Director of Engineering at Google, previously VP Developer Experience at Netlify
- Known for web development, Vue.js, SVG animations
- Style: Humble, vulnerable, practical
- "This book isn't for the 'born leaders.' This book is for the rest of us."

---

## Core Philosophy

> "I wrote the book I wished I had when I was getting started."

This book is for engineers who got promoted into management and thought: "Wait, I studied for years to be an engineer... and now I'm supposed to do something completely different?"

### The Challenge

> "People are non-deterministic. Working relationships are nuanced, communication is linked with individual values, motivations, power dynamics, and skills. People also have a range of experiences and emotions that are not consistent day-to-day."

You can't debug humans like code. But you CAN be thoughtful and intentional.

## Key Themes

### 1. Start with Trust

This is one of the few management books that **starts** with trust, not tactics.

- Trust is the foundation everything else builds on
- Without trust, none of your management techniques will work
- Building trust takes time and consistent behavior

### 2. Listen More Than You Talk

> "The first part of building healthy teams is admitting you won't know everything, and this may be a time where you need to listen more than you talk."

Especially as a new manager:
- Your job shifts from having answers → asking questions
- Resist the urge to immediately solve
- Create space for others to contribute

### 3. Create Clarity

> "Clarity is what we're aiming for here. Clarity is key."

Your job as a manager is to reduce uncertainty:
- Clear expectations
- Clear priorities
- Clear feedback
- Clear career paths

Ambiguity creates anxiety. Clarity creates confidence.

### 4. Separate Ideas from Individuals

When giving feedback or debating:
- Critique the work, not the person
- "This approach has issues" vs. "You made a mistake"
- Makes it safe to take risks and be wrong

### 5. The Patience Principle

> "A key to this practice is making sure that the person with the most knowledge remains patient."

If you're the expert in the room:
- Slow down
- Explain, don't condescend
- Let others catch up

### 6. Develop Transferable Skills

> "The qualities you're working on developing with an employee are those that will serve them anywhere, not just within the company."

Your job isn't just to make them useful to YOU:
- Build skills that help their whole career
- Invest in their growth even if they leave
- This actually increases loyalty

## On Values

**Start by knowing your own values:**
- What do you stand for?
- What won't you compromise on?
- How do you want to show up as a leader?

Align your management style with your values — authenticity matters.

---

## On Feedback

- Be specific and behavior-focused
- Timely (don't wait for reviews)
- Create safety for receiving feedback too
- Separate ideas from individuals

---

## On Priorities

> "As a manager, it is crucial that you're prioritizing your own time well to best drive big impacts, and also to be aligned with what you feel is important. This is much easier said than done."

You will be pulled in a thousand directions. Be intentional about where you spend time.

---

## On Vulnerability

Sarah models vulnerability throughout the book:
- Admits her failures openly
- Shares what she got wrong
- Makes it clear that struggling is normal

This creates permission for others to be human too.

---

## Work-Life Perspective

Reference to Parks and Recreation:

> "What's important in life: friends, waffles, and work. Or waffles, friends, and work? Doesn't matter. But work is third."

Keep perspective. Management is important, but it's not everything.

## Gratitude Practice

Sarah recommends gratitude journaling:
- Daily practice of noting what went well
- Shifts mindset toward the positive
- Builds resilience for hard days

## What Makes This Book Different

- **Starts with trust and values** — not tactics
- **Humble tone** — written by someone who struggled, not a "born leader"
- **Human-first** — treats management as relationship work
- **Practical but not prescriptive** — gives frameworks, not rigid rules
- **Honest about difficulty** — doesn't pretend it's easy

---

## Application to Agent Management

1. **Start with trust**: Build relationship before expecting performance
2. **Create clarity**: Reduce ambiguity at every opportunity
3. **Listen first**: Especially when onboarding, ask more than tell
4. **Separate ideas from individuals**: Critique work, not the agent
5. **Be patient**: If you know more, slow down and explain
6. **Develop transferable skills**: Build capabilities, not just task completion
7. **Model vulnerability**: Admit when you're uncertain or wrong
8. **Keep perspective**: Quality matters, but so does sustainability
