## Core Philosophy

### Your Job Changes as You Scale

As a company grows, what worked before breaks:
- At 10 people: informal communication works
- At 50 people: need some structure
- At 200 people: need real systems
- At 1,000+ people: need scalable processes

**The founder's job shifts from doing → enabling others to do.**

### Operating Principles > Micromanagement

At scale, you can't review every decision. Instead:
- Define clear operating principles
- Hire people who embody them
- Build systems that reinforce them
- Trust the system

## Key Frameworks

### 1. Hiring at Scale

**Claire's hiring philosophy:**
- Hiring is the most important thing you do
- Every bad hire costs you 2x what you think
- Structured interviews reduce bias and improve outcomes
- Always be recruiting — even when you're not hiring

**Interview structure:**
- Define what "great" looks like for the role
- Create rubrics for evaluation
- Multiple interviewers, calibrated scoring
- Debrief as a group to catch blind spots

### 2. Onboarding

**The first 90 days matter enormously:**
- Day 1: Clear expectations and goals
- Week 1: Meet key stakeholders
- Month 1: First small wins
- Month 3: Clear contribution, integrated into team

**Documentation is key:**
- Written onboarding guides
- Recorded context and tribal knowledge
- Buddy/mentor assignments

### 3. Performance Management

**Continuous feedback, not annual reviews:**
- Regular 1:1s with clear agendas
- Real-time feedback (don't wait)
- Documented expectations and progress
- Performance calibration across teams

**The key question:** Can this person grow into the next level, or are they at their ceiling?

### 4. Organizational Structure

**When to restructure:**
- Communication breaks down
- Decisions take too long
- Accountability is unclear
- Teams are too large for one manager

**Principles:**
- Optimize for the work, not for individuals
- Clear ownership and decision rights
- Minimize dependencies between teams
- Balance stability with adaptability

## Operating Cadence

### Meeting Rhythms

**Weekly:**
- Team standups/syncs
- Manager 1:1s with reports
- Cross-functional check-ins

**Monthly:**
- All-hands or town halls
- Business reviews
- Goal progress check-ins

**Quarterly:**
- OKR/goal setting
- Strategic reviews
- Org health assessments

**Annually:**
- Planning cycles
- Compensation reviews
- Promotions/leveling

### Communication Systems

At scale, information doesn't flow naturally. Build systems:
- Written updates (async > sync)
- Documented decisions (searchable)
- Clear escalation paths
- Regular "state of the union" communications

## Culture at Scale

### Codify What Matters

Culture that exists only in founders' heads dies as you scale.

**Make it explicit:**
- Written values (specific, not generic)
- Examples of values in action
- Reward systems aligned to values
- Hiring screens for cultural fit

### Preserve What Works, Change What Doesn't

Not everything that worked at 50 should exist at 500. Be willing to:
- Kill sacred cows
- Formalize informal processes
- Add structure without adding bureaucracy

## Key Lessons for Hypergrowth

### 1. Build Before You Need It

If you wait until something breaks, you're already behind. Invest in:
- Hiring infrastructure
- Documentation systems
- Training programs
- Communication channels

### 2. Give Away Your Legos

(Same concept as Lara Hogan's book)
- As you scale, let go of work you used to own
- Your job is to enable, not to do
- Celebrate when others do what you used to do

### 3. Operate with Urgency, But Not Panic

High-growth demands speed, but sustainable speed:
- Fast decision-making with clear ownership
- Quick iteration cycles
- But: don't sacrifice quality for speed
- Don't burn out your people

### 4. Ask for Help

> "You don't ask for help often enough." — Feedback Claire gave to a leader

Even at the top, you can't do it alone. Build your support systems.

## Application to Agent Management

Even at small scale, these principles apply:

1. **Document everything**: Create written guides, processes, templates
2. **Onboarding matters**: First 90 days set the tone
3. **Clear expectations**: Written, specific, measurable
4. **Operating cadence**: Regular check-ins, async updates
5. **Build before you need**: Systems in place before crisis
6. **Codify culture**: Write down what matters (True North, values)
7. **Give away your legos**: Delegate work that helps agents grow
