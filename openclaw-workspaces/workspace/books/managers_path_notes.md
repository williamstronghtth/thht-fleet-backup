# The Manager's Path - Camille Fournier
**A Guide for Tech Leaders Navigating Growth and Change**

## About the Author
- Camille Fournier: former CTO at Rent the Runway
- Heavy contributor to Apache Zookeeper
- Book covers the full career ladder: IC → Tech Lead → Manager → Director → VP → CTO
- Published March 2017, 244 pages

---

## The Career Ladder

The book walks through each stage of the engineering career path, highlighting that **every level requires a fundamentally different skillset** — what got you here won't get you there.

---

## Key Themes

### 1. Technical vs. Non-Technical Balance

As you move up:
- **IC:** Mostly technical work
- **Tech Lead:** 50/50 balance
- **Manager:** More communication, strategy, planning
- **Senior Leader:** Mostly non-technical (vision, alignment, culture)

Finding the right balance is hard and it's a **moving target** throughout your career.

### 2. Your Words Carry More Weight

> "Most developers are used to brainstorming and challenging ideas as normal. When you become a leader, people interpret the same feedback as marching orders."

**As a leader:**
- Make it clear: "must do" vs. "just an idea to consider"
- Sometimes you need to keep your mouth shut entirely
- Thinking out loud can derail projects

### 3. True North

As a tech lead, define "true north" for your team:
- 100% code coverage?
- Continuous deployment?
- Five 9's reliability?
- Sub-second load times?

**You can't do all of these at once.** Pick one, make sure everyone knows it, and use it to simplify every decision.

## Role-by-Role Insights

### Individual Contributor (IC)

- **Your peers become your future jobs** — network matters
- Be proactive: When unhappy, say something. When stuck, ask for help. When you want a raise, ask.
- Understand what your manager deals with — it makes you a better employee

### Tech Lead

> "Tech lead is not the job for the person who wants to focus deeply on their own code."

- **Influencing without authority** — you lead the team but report to the same manager
- You must influence peers AND influence up
- Don't give this role to the "best coder" — give it to someone who can lead
- **Delegation ≠ Abdication** — you're still responsible for success

### Manager of a Team

> "At every step beyond this level you will experience a totally different set of requirements and challenges."

**The hardest adjustment:** You're doing totally different things. Management is NOT a natural progression of senior engineer skills.

**Missing quick wins:**
> "Writing code is full of quick wins. Management has fewer obvious quick wins. It's natural to feel longing for simpler times when it was just you and your computer."

### Regular 1-on-1s

> "Regular 1-1s are like oil changes; if you skip them, plan to get stranded on the side of the highway at the worst possible time." — Marc Hedlund

**Best practices:**
- Don't use them as pure status meetings
- Keep a running document of notes from each meeting
- These are for THEM, not for you

### The Rule of No Surprises

> "One of the basic rules of management is the rule of no surprises, particularly negative ones."

- Bad news should never blindside your boss
- If something's going wrong, surface it early
- This builds trust

## Saying No to Your Boss

> "Saying no rarely looks like a simple 'no' when you're a manager."

**Use the "Yes, and" technique:**
- "Yes, we can do that project, AND all we'll need to do is delay the start of this other project."

**Respond with positivity while articulating reality.** This is what gets you into senior leadership.

---

## Communication at Scale

> "Never underestimate how many times and how many ways something needs to be said before it sinks in."

**Things need to be said at least 3 times** before they sink in:
- Different formats (email, meeting, 1-on-1)
- Different framings
- Repetition is not nagging — it's necessary

## On Process

> "A good political idea is one that works well in half-baked form."

Same for engineering processes:
- Process should have value even when not followed perfectly
- The value lies in socializing change/risk to the team

## Application to Agent Management

For managing the software engineer agent:

1. **True North:** Define one clear priority (speed? quality? learning?)
2. **Words carry weight:** Be explicit about "must do" vs. "consider this"
3. **1-on-1s matter:** Regular check-ins, keep notes, track progress
4. **No surprises:** Create culture where bad news surfaces early
5. **Delegation ≠ Abdication:** Stay involved enough to help them succeed
6. **Communication repetition:** Say important things 3+ times, different ways
7. **"Yes, and":** When pushing back, offer alternatives not just objections
