## Core Philosophy

> "Tell less and ask more. Your advice is not as good as you think it is."

The problem: Most leaders give too much advice. This creates dependency, not growth.

The solution: Replace advice-giving with question-asking.

---

## The Advice Monster

We all have an "Advice Monster" — the urge to immediately solve problems for people.

**Why advice-giving is a trap:**
- Creates dependency, not autonomy
- Makes you a bottleneck
- Robs them of learning
- Your advice often isn't as good as you think

> "I can tell you something, and it's got a limited chance of making its way into your brain. If I can ask you a question and you generate the answer yourself, the odds increase substantially."

## The Seven Essential Questions

### 1. The Kickstart Question
**"What's on your mind?"**

- Opens the conversation fast
- Skip the small talk
- Gets to what matters

Often the answer falls into one of **3Ps**:
- **Projects** — the work itself
- **People** — relationships/conflicts
- **Patterns** — recurring behaviors

### 2. The AWE Question
**"And what else?"**

- The best coaching question in the world
- Draws out more information
- Buys you time to think
- Keeps the Advice Monster at bay
- Often the first answer isn't the real answer

**Use it 3-4 times per conversation.**

### 3. The Focus Question
**"What's the real challenge here for you?"**

- Cuts through vagueness
- Makes it personal and specific
- Prevents "coaching the ghost" (talking about someone who's not there)
- Helps identify the ONE thing that matters most

### 4. The Foundation Question
**"What do you want?"**

- Often people don't actually know
- Clarifies the real goal
- Enables adult conversation: "Yes," "No," "Let me think," or "Not that, but this"
- Distinguishes wants from needs (needs = the reasons behind wants)

### 5. The Lazy Question
**"How can I help?"**

- Forces them to make a direct request
- Prevents you from being the perpetual rescuer
- Blunt version: "What do you want from me?"
- "Lazy" because it saves you from providing unwanted help

### 6. The Strategic Question
**"If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"**

- Every yes is a no to something else
- Prevents overcommitment
- Forces prioritization
- Helps them own their choices

### 7. The Learning Question
**"What was most useful for you?"**

- Closes the conversation with reflection
- Reinforces learning
- Debriefing is where real learning happens
- Side benefit: Makes you seem more useful

## The Drama Triangle (Karpman)

A toxic pattern in relationships:

```
    PERSECUTOR ←——→ RESCUER
         ↖       ↗
          VICTIM
```

**Three dysfunctional roles:**
- **Victim**: "Poor me, I can't handle this"
- **Persecutor**: "It's your fault, you're the problem"
- **Rescuer**: "Let me save you, I'll fix it"

**The trap:** Even "Rescuer" is dysfunctional — it creates dependency and disempowers the other person.

**The solution:** Stay out of the triangle. Ask questions instead of rescuing.

## Question Masterclass Tips

### How to Ask Better:
1. **Cut the intro** — just ask the question
2. **Stick to "What" questions** — "Why" can feel judgmental
3. **Get comfortable with silence** — let them think
4. **Listen to the full answer** — don't interrupt
5. **Acknowledge the answer** — before asking another question

### What NOT to do:
- Don't ask questions with advice attached ("Have you thought about...?")
- Don't ask leading questions (you already know the answer you want)
- Don't ask multiple questions at once

## Building the Coaching Habit

Use the habit loop from Charles Duhigg's "The Power of Habit":

1. **Trigger**: What activates the old behavior?
2. **Old Habit**: What do you usually do? (give advice)
3. **New Habit**: What will you do instead? (ask a question)

For each of the seven questions, identify:
- When will you use it?
- What trigger will remind you?
- What old habit will it replace?

## Application to Agent Management

1. **Resist the Advice Monster**: When an agent asks a question, ask one back first
2. **Use "And what else?"**: Often the first answer isn't the real issue
3. **Stay out of Rescuer mode**: Don't just fix things — help them learn to fix things
4. **"What do you want?"**: Clarify what success looks like before diving in
5. **Close with learning**: "What was most useful?" cements growth
6. **What questions > Why questions**: "What happened?" not "Why did you do that?"
