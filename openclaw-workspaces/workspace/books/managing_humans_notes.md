## The Rands Test (Team Health Scorecard)

Inspired by Joel Spolsky's "Joel Test" — a quick diagnostic for engineering team health.

### Score Your Team (+1 for each Yes, -1 for status reports):

1. **Do you have consistent 1:1s that aren't just status?** (+1)
   - 1:1s should be STRATEGIC, not tactical
   - First meeting to cancel when things hit the fan = wrong move
   - "A 1:1 is a weekly investment in the individuals that make up your team"

2. **Do you have a consistent team meeting?** (+1)
   - Standing agenda item: "Gossip, rumors, and lies" — kill the grapevine
   - Did you make tangible progress on something?

3. **Are status reports delivered via email weekly?** (-1)
   - "Email-based status reports are one of the clearest signs of managerial incompetence"
   - Status already exists in your tools (version control, bug tracking, etc.)
   - Status reports say: "You don't value my time"

4. **Can you say NO to your boss?** (+1)
   - If you can't speak your mind, something is broken
   - Leaders who think they're infallible slowly go insane with power
   - "Your contractual obligation as a shareholder is to raise your hand and say 'That's stupid. Here's why...'"

5. **Can you explain company strategy to a stranger?** (+1)
   - Do you have a clear map of the company in your head?
   - If you can't explain it, you can't measure it

6. **Can you tell me the state of the business?** (+1)
   - Your company is either growing or dying
   - Have a defensible opinion on which

7. **Does leadership regularly stand up and share what they're thinking?** (+1)
   - Essential at any size
   - Gives everyone access to vision

8. **Are you buying what leadership is selling?** (+1)
   - Does their version of truth match yours?
   - If not, what does that say about the business?

9. **Can you explain your career trajectory?** (+1)
   - Know your next move
   - Bonus: Does your boss know what you want to do next? (+1)

10. **Do you have protected time to be strategic?** (+1)
    - Not just doing — thinking about where you're going

11. **Are you actively killing the Grapevine?** (+1)
    - Rumors fill information vacuums
    - Your job: fill the vacuum with truth

## Key Concepts

### Information Starvation
- When people don't get information, they make it up
- Rumors are symptoms of information vacuum
- Fix: over-communicate, be transparent

### The Update, The Vent, and The Disaster

Three types of 1:1 conversations:

1. **The Update**: Status-oriented, low-value if that's all you do
2. **The Vent**: They need to blow off steam — LISTEN, don't solve
3. **The Disaster**: Something is seriously wrong — time for action

Know which one you're in. Don't try to solve a Vent.

### Meeting Player Types

Identify who's in the room:
- **The Anchor**: Runs the meeting, keeps it on track
- **Chatty Patty**: Won't stop talking, needs to be managed
- **Mr. Irrelevant**: Contributes nothing useful
- **The Jerk**: Actively derails for ego

Your job: know who's playing what role and adjust accordingly.

### Engineer Archetypes

**Stable vs. Volatile:**
- **Stable**: Predictable, reliable, steady output
- **Volatile**: Brilliant but unpredictable — high highs, low lows

Both are valuable. Manage them differently.

**Free Electrons:**
- Self-directed senior engineers who can figure out anything
- Give them hard problems and get out of the way
- Rare and precious — don't micromanage them

**Completionists vs. Incrementalists:**
- **Completionists**: Want to finish the whole thing perfectly
- **Incrementalists**: Ship early, iterate often

Both are right. Help them understand each other.

### In Silence, You Can Assess

- When things get heated, stop talking
- Silence creates space to think
- "In silence you can assess" — observe, don't react

## On 1:1s

> "Regular 1:1s are the single best investment you can make in your team."

**Rules:**
- Never cancel them
- Don't make them pure status
- Let them vent when needed
- Make them feel heard

**The Vent Warning:**
When someone starts venting, your instinct is to fix it. DON'T.
- Let them get it out
- Ask: "Is this something you want me to help fix, or do you just need to vent?"
- Sometimes listening IS the job

---

## On Meetings

### Kill Bad Meetings

- Every meeting needs an owner (The Anchor)
- If you're not adding value, leave
- A meeting with no decision is a waste

### Agenda Detection

Walk into any meeting and ask: what are we actually trying to accomplish?
- If no one can answer, the meeting is broken
- Your job: identify the real purpose (even if hidden)

---

## The No Surprises Rule

> "One of the basic rules of management is the rule of no surprises, particularly negative ones."

- Bad news should never blindside your boss
- If something's going wrong, surface it early
- Don't let your boss learn bad news from someone else

---

## On Boredom

If your best people are bored, they're about to leave.

**Signs of boredom:**
- Cynicism
- Checking out in meetings
- Side projects taking priority

**Fix:**
- Give them new challenges
- Move them to different problems
- Let them learn something new

## Application to Agent Management

1. **The Rands Test**: Use it as a health check for your agent team
2. **No email status reports**: Track work in tools (GitHub, CRM, etc.)
3. **1:1s via Telegram**: Keep them conversational, not bureaucratic
4. **Know your archetypes**: Is the agent Stable or Volatile? Completionist or Incrementalist?
5. **Kill the grapevine**: Over-communicate decisions and reasoning
6. **Watch for boredom**: If an agent seems checked out, give them a harder problem
7. **In silence, assess**: When debugging issues, observe before reacting
