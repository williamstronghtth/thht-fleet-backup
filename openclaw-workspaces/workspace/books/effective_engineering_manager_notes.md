## The Book's Structure

Three levels of mastery:
1. **Managing Yourself** — Personal effectiveness as a manager
2. **Managing Your Team** — Leading and growing people
3. **Managing the Department & Beyond** — Organizational impact

---

## Part 1: Managing Yourself

### Transition from Engineer to Manager
- Different job entirely — not a promotion, a **career change**
- Your output is now **your team's output**, not your code
- Success metric changes: from "What did I build?" to "What did my team achieve?"

### Information Management
- Managers deal with **10x more information** than ICs
- Develop systems for:
  - Note-taking (1:1s, meetings, decisions)
  - Task tracking
  - Context switching
- "Your brain is for having ideas, not holding them"

### Delegation (Not Micromanagement)
- **Delegate outcomes, not tasks**
- Give context, not instructions
- Trust but verify — check-ins, not check-ups
- Let people fail safely — that's how they learn

### Managing Your Boss
- Make their job easier
- Keep them informed (no surprises)
- Understand their goals and priorities
- "Manage up" — proactively communicate status and blockers

## Part 2: Managing Your Team

### Hiring & Building Teams
- Hire for **growth potential**, not just current skills
- Culture fit matters — skills can be taught, attitude can't
- Diverse teams outperform homogeneous ones
- Onboarding sets the tone — invest in it heavily

### Firing & Difficult Conversations
- Never a surprise — if you've been giving feedback all along
- Document everything
- Be direct, be kind, be quick
- Protect the team from toxic individuals

### Performance Reviews & Feedback
- Continuous feedback > annual reviews
- Specific, actionable, timely
- Balance praise and constructive criticism
- Tie feedback to impact and growth

### Coaching & Mentoring
- **Coaching**: Asking questions to help them find answers
- **Mentoring**: Sharing your experience and advice
- Know when to use each
- Create psychological safety — people need to feel safe to fail

### Keeping People Happy While Shipping
- Sustainable pace > crunch
- Celebrate wins (big and small)
- Shield team from unnecessary chaos
- Autonomy, mastery, purpose — the motivation trifecta

### Handling Pressure & Deadlines
- Don't pass stress down — absorb it, filter it
- Negotiate scope, not quality
- Communicate trade-offs clearly
- Protect the team's focus

---

## Part 3: Managing the Department

### Working Across Teams
- Break down silos
- Build relationships before you need them
- Guilds & communities of practice — share knowledge
- Clear interfaces and communication channels

### Career Tracks
- **IC (Individual Contributor) track**: Senior → Staff → Principal
- **Management track**: Lead → Manager → Director
- Both tracks should have equal prestige
- Help people find their path

### Remote & Flexible Work
- Output > hours
- Over-communicate in remote settings
- Create virtual watercooler moments
- Trust is essential

### Diversity & Inclusion
- Active effort required — it doesn't happen naturally
- Examine your biases
- Create inclusive environments
- Diverse perspectives = better solutions

## Application: Managing Our Software Engineer Agent

**When we hire next week, I'll apply:**

1. **Clear Outcomes** — Define what success looks like, not step-by-step instructions
2. **Context Over Instructions** — Explain the "why" so they can make good decisions
3. **Regular Check-Ins** — 1:1 syncs to review progress, remove blockers
4. **Feedback Loop** — Continuous improvement, not just at review time
5. **Psychological Safety** — Safe to ask questions, make mistakes, learn
6. **Autonomy** — Trust them to figure out the "how"
7. **Shield from Chaos** — Filter noise, let them focus on their work
