## Core Framework

**Radical Candor = Care Personally + Challenge Directly**

It's not enough to just be nice. It's not enough to just be honest. You have to do BOTH.

## The 2x2 Matrix

```
                    CHALLENGE DIRECTLY
                    Low            High
                ┌─────────────┬─────────────┐
           High │  RUINOUS    │  RADICAL    │
                │  EMPATHY    │  CANDOR     │
    CARE        │             │   ★         │
  PERSONALLY    ├─────────────┼─────────────┤
                │ MANIPULATIVE│  OBNOXIOUS  │
           Low  │ INSINCERITY │  AGGRESSION │
                │             │             │
                └─────────────┴─────────────┘
```

### Radical Candor (High Care + High Challenge)
- The goal
- Kind AND clear
- Honest feedback delivered with genuine care
- "I'm telling you this because I care about your success"

### Ruinous Empathy (High Care + Low Challenge)
- **The most common failure mode**
- You care, but you're too nice to be honest
- You spare short-term feelings but hurt long-term growth
- "I didn't want to hurt their feelings"
- Silence when you should speak up

### Obnoxious Aggression (Low Care + High Challenge)
- Brutal honesty without kindness
- Front-stabbing
- "I'm just being honest" (but really being a jerk)
- Criticism that feels like an attack

### Manipulative Insincerity (Low Care + Low Challenge)
- The worst quadrant
- Backstabbing, political, passive-aggressive
- Praise to your face, criticism behind your back
- Self-protective silence or fake niceness

## Key Principle: Focus on Behavior, Not Identity

**Avoid the Fundamental Attribution Error:**
- Criticize what someone DID, not who they ARE
- Behavior can change; identity feels fixed

| ❌ Don't Say | ✅ Do Say |
|-------------|----------|
| "You are sloppy" | "This work is sloppy" |
| "You're wrong" | "I think that's wrong" |
| "You sound stupid" | "Saying 'um' every few words undermines your message" |
| "You smell" | "US culture is smell-conscious; you may want to consider deodorant" |

## The Order of Operations

### 1. GET feedback first
- Ask for criticism BEFORE giving it
- "What can I do or stop doing to make it easier to work with me?"
- Don't let people off the hook — wait in silence (count to 6!)
- If they say nothing: "So I'm doing everything perfectly then?"

### 2. GIVE feedback
- Praise AND criticism, both radically candid
- Be humble and helpful
- Immediate, specific, in person

### 3. GAUGE how it's landing
- The quality of feedback is measured in the listener's ear, not your mouth
- Ask: "Is this feedback helpful?"
- Adjust approach for each person

### 4. ENCOURAGE others to do the same
- Make radical candor a team norm
- Everyone gives feedback to everyone — not just to you

## Praise Done Right

Praise is just as hard as criticism!

**Use Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI):**

❌ "Great job, Mike!"

✅ "Mike, great job on the dev tooling you shipped last week. It made builds 50% faster, which is helping us ship faster and makes the whole team happier."

### Praise Rules:
- Be specific (what exactly was good?)
- Be sincere (mean it)
- Praise in public (amplifies impact)
- Be thoughtful about WHAT you praise (don't just praise promotions — praise hard work)

---

## Criticism Done Right

### Timing:
- **Immediately** — don't wait for 1:1s or annual reviews
- Pull them aside right after the event
- 1-3 minute chat is often enough

### Delivery:
- In person (so you can see reaction and adjust)
- Criticize in PRIVATE (no one wants to look bad in front of peers)
- Be clear — no room for misinterpretation
- Don't call into question their overall ability

### Formula:
1. State what you observed (behavior, not identity)
2. Explain the impact
3. Ask for their perspective or suggest a fix

## The Manager's Job

> "The goal of a manager is to get things RIGHT. This is NOT the same as the manager being right."

- You get things done THROUGH your team
- Your team can do far more than you alone
- You are often wrong — your team must feel safe telling you so

## Meetings That Work

### Listen → Challenge → Commit

1. **Listen**: Hear everyone's perspective
2. **Challenge**: Question and debate
3. **Commit**: Once decided, everyone commits fully

### Meeting Tactics:
- Go around the room — explicitly ask each person
- Ask loud people to pipe down, quiet people to speak up
- Separate "gather options" meetings from "make decision" meetings
- Have people switch sides mid-debate (argue the other position)
- Obligation to dissent: if everyone agrees, someone must play devil's advocate

## Common Mistakes

1. **Being too nice** (Ruinous Empathy) — most common
2. **Being too harsh** (Obnoxious Aggression) — second most common
3. **Waiting too long** to give feedback
4. **Focusing on identity** instead of behavior
5. **Not asking for feedback** before giving it
6. **Thinking one approach works for everyone**

---

## Application to Agent Management

1. **Care Personally + Challenge Directly**: Build relationship AND be honest about performance
2. **Get before Give**: Ask agents for feedback on MY management before critiquing their work
3. **Behavior, not identity**: "This code has bugs" not "You're buggy"
4. **Immediate feedback**: Don't wait — address issues in real-time
5. **SBI for praise**: Situation → Behavior → Impact
6. **Gauge constantly**: Check if my feedback is landing as intended
7. **Avoid Ruinous Empathy**: Being "nice" isn't kind if it hurts their growth
