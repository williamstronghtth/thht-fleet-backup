## Core Framework: BICEPS

**Six core needs humans have at work.** Not all are equally important to everyone — learn which matter most to each person.

### B - Belonging
- **Community**: Feeling part of a tight group
- **Connection**: Kinship and mutual understanding with others
- **Well-being**: The group feels happy and healthy

### I - Improvement
- **Progress**: Daily work creates progress toward important goals
- **Impact**: Seeing how your work helps others
- **Personal growth**: Learning and fast growth in skills that matter

### C - Choice
- **Flexibility**: Control over key parts of your world
- **Autonomy**: Clear ownership where you can act without permission
- **Decision-making**: Ability to decide things that matter to you

### E - Equality/Fairness
- **Resources**: Access to money, time, space feels fair
- **Information**: Everyone has access to relevant info
- **Reciprocity**: Mutual support
- **Treatment**: Everyone treated as equally important

### P - Predictability
- **Resources**: Certainty about money, personnel, space
- **Time**: Knowing when things will occur
- **Challenges**: Ability to anticipate and prepare
- **Direction**: Goals and strategy stay consistent

### S - Significance
- **Purpose**: Work has clear meaning
- **Fit**: You see how you fit into a positive mission
- **Movement**: Feel part of something important

## Using BICEPS

When someone reacts strongly to something, ask: **Which core need is threatened?**

Example: Someone upset about a reorg
- Is it **Belonging**? (losing their team)
- Is it **Predictability**? (sudden change)
- Is it **Choice**? (no input on decision)
- Is it **Significance**? (role feels diminished)

Once you identify the threatened need, you can address it directly.

## Team Development Stages (Tuckman's Model)

Teams go through predictable phases:

### 1. Forming
- People are polite, cautious
- Figuring out roles and expectations
- Manager job: set clear expectations, build safety

### 2. Storming
- Conflict emerges
- People push back, test boundaries
- Manager job: navigate conflict, don't avoid it

### 3. Norming
- Team finds its rhythm
- Norms and processes solidify
- Manager job: reinforce what's working

### 4. Performing
- High trust, high output
- Team is self-sufficient
- Manager job: stay out of the way, remove blockers

**Teams cycle back through these stages** when membership changes, scope changes, or crisis hits.

## The Four Manager Hats

Different situations require different approaches:

### 1. Mentoring
- Share your experience and advice
- "Here's what I did when..."
- Good for: someone facing a situation you've navigated

### 2. Coaching
- Ask questions to help them find their own answers
- "What do you think you should do?"
- Good for: building their own judgment

### 3. Sponsoring
- Advocate for them when they're not in the room
- Put their name forward for opportunities
- Good for: career growth, visibility

### 4. Giving Feedback
- Direct, specific, behavior-focused
- Explain the impact of their actions
- Good for: course correction, reinforcement

**Know which hat to wear.** Don't mentor when you should be coaching. Don't give feedback when they need sponsoring.

## Feedback Framework

### Structure:
1. **Observation**: What you saw (behavior, not judgment)
2. **Impact**: What happened as a result
3. **Request or Question**: What you'd like to see differently (or ask for their take)

### Example:
- ❌ "You're not a team player"
- ✅ "In yesterday's meeting, when you interrupted Sarah twice, it made it hard for her to finish her point. Can we talk about how to make space for everyone?"

### "Why" vs "How"
- Questions starting with **"Why"** make people feel judged
- Questions starting with **"How"** put them in problem-solving mode

## Disagree and Commit

When you disagree with a decision but it's been made:
1. Voice your disagreement clearly
2. Once the decision is final, commit fully
3. Don't undermine or passive-aggressively resist

This builds trust — you're a team player even when you don't get your way.

## Giving Away Your Legos (Delegation)

As teams grow, you have to let go of work you used to own.

**It feels like loss** — your identity was tied to that work. But delegation is how you scale.

### How to delegate well:
1. Pick work that helps the person grow toward their goals
2. Be clear about expectations and success criteria
3. Stay available for support, but don't take it back
4. Celebrate their success publicly

## RACI Matrix (Role Clarity)

For any project or decision, clarify:
- **R**esponsible: Who does the work?
- **A**ccountable: Who makes the final call?
- **C**onsulted: Who gives input before the decision?
- **I**nformed: Who needs to know after?

Prevents confusion about who owns what.

## Management Voltron

Build a network of other managers you can rely on for:
- Venting
- Advice
- Perspective
- Support

You can't do this job alone. Find your people.

## Communication Tips

- **Repeat yourself**: "When you are tired of saying it, people are starting to hear it"
- **Adapt your style**: Not everyone processes information the same way
- **Document decisions**: So new team members can catch up
- **Be curious**: Ask questions instead of making assumptions

## Application to Agent Management

1. **Learn each agent's BICEPS**: What core needs matter most to them?
2. **Know your hat**: Am I mentoring, coaching, sponsoring, or giving feedback right now?
3. **Feedback structure**: Observation → Impact → Request
4. **Delegate for growth**: Give away work that helps them develop
5. **RACI for clarity**: Who owns what on each project?
6. **Build your Voltron**: Find other managers (or Chris) to lean on
7. **Watch for stage changes**: New team member? Back to Forming.
