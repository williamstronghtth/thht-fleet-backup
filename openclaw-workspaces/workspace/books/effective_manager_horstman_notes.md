## The Core Philosophy

Mark Horstman's approach is brutally practical: **Management is about getting results through your team.** Period.

The book is based on 25+ years of research and the Manager Tools podcast — one of the most popular management podcasts ever. It's not theory; it's field-tested, actionable guidance.

---

## The Manager Tools "Trinity" + Coaching

The entire book centers on **four core behaviors** that, if done consistently, will make you effective:

### 1. 🗣️ One-on-Ones (O3s)
### 2. 📊 Feedback
### 3. 📥 Delegation
### 4. 🎯 Coaching

---

## 1. One-on-Ones (O3s)

**The most important tool in a manager's toolkit.**

### The Rules:
- **Weekly** — non-negotiable, same time every week
- **30 minutes** — no more, no less
- **Their meeting, not yours** — direct's agenda first

### The Format:
| Time | Focus |
|------|-------|
| First 10 min | Direct's topics (whatever they want to discuss) |
| Second 10 min | Manager's topics (your agenda) |
| Last 10 min | Future/development (career, growth, projects) |

### Why It Works:
- Builds trust through consistent contact
- Catches problems early (small issues don't become big ones)
- Shows you care about THEM, not just their output
- Creates space for informal communication

### Key Quotes:
> "The purpose of a one-on-one is to build a relationship."

> "If you don't have time for one-on-ones, you don't have time to be a manager."

## 2. Feedback

**The breakfast of champions.**

### Two Types:
1. **Positive Feedback** — Reinforce good behaviors (do MORE of this!)
2. **Negative/Adjusting Feedback** — Correct poor behaviors (do LESS of this)

### The Feedback Model:
1. **Ask** — "Can I give you some feedback?"
2. **Describe the behavior** — What you saw (specific, observable)
3. **State the impact** — What happened because of it
4. **Encourage/Request change** — Keep doing it OR please change

### Examples:

**Positive:**
> "Can I give you some feedback? When you sent that status update without being asked, it made the client feel confident we're on top of things. Great job — keep doing that!"

**Adjusting:**
> "Can I give you some feedback? When you missed the deadline without letting me know, I couldn't update the client and it hurt our credibility. In the future, can you flag delays as soon as you see them?"

### Key Rules:
- **Ratio: 10:1** — 10 positive for every 1 negative
- **Immediate** — Feedback loses power over time
- **Specific** — About behavior, not character
- **Brief** — Don't lecture, make the point and move on

> "Feedback is not about you being right. It's about making them successful."

## 3. Delegation

**Push work DOWN to develop your team and free yourself for higher-value work.**

### Why Managers Don't Delegate:
- "It's faster if I do it myself" (short-term thinking)
- "They won't do it as well" (perfectionism)
- "I don't want to burden them" (misplaced kindness)

### The Delegation Model:
1. **State the task** — What needs to be done
2. **Explain the importance** — Why it matters (context!)
3. **Specify the deadline** — When it's due
4. **Ask for understanding** — "Can you tell me what I'm asking?"
5. **Identify obstacles** — "What might get in the way?"
6. **Follow up** — Check progress before the deadline

### Levels of Delegation:
| Level | Autonomy | Check-In |
|-------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Research and report back | Before any action |
| 2 | Research, recommend, then act | Before acting |
| 3 | Act, then report | After acting |
| 4 | Act, report only if problem | Minimal |
| 5 | Full ownership | None needed |

### Key Insight:
> "You can delegate authority, but you cannot delegate responsibility."

You're still accountable for the outcome — but they do the work.

---

## 4. Coaching

**Help them improve over time through systematic development.**

### Coaching vs. Feedback:
- **Feedback** = Short-term, about a specific behavior
- **Coaching** = Long-term, about developing capabilities

### The Coaching Model:
1. **Collaborate on goals** — What does the direct want to improve?
2. **Collaborate on resources** — Training, mentorship, stretch assignments
3. **Collaborate on a plan** — Specific actions with timelines
4. **Follow up relentlessly** — Accountability through O3s

### Coaching Questions:
- "Where do you want to be in 2 years?"
- "What skills do you need to get there?"
- "What's one thing we can work on this quarter?"
- "How can I help?"

## The Manager's Job

Horstman defines a manager's job as:

> **"Get results. Retain your people."**

That's it. Everything else flows from these two goals.

### The Priority Order:
1. **Results** — You exist to deliver outcomes
2. **Retention** — Keep your good people (replacing them is expensive)
3. **Development** — Grow their capabilities
4. **Your comfort** — Last priority (get over yourself)

---

## Implementation Order

Horstman recommends rolling out the tools in this order:

| Week | Add This |
|------|----------|
| 1-4 | One-on-Ones (build the relationship foundation) |
| 5-8 | Positive Feedback only (build trust) |
| 9-12 | Add Adjusting Feedback (now they trust you) |
| 13+ | Delegation and Coaching (they're ready) |

> "Don't start giving negative feedback until you've been doing positive feedback for weeks. They need to know you see the good too."
