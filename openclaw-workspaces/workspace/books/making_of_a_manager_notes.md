# The Making of a Manager - Julie Zhuo
**What to Do When Everyone Looks to You**

## About the Author
- Julie Zhuo was VP of Product Design at Facebook
- Became a manager at 25 with no idea what she was doing
- Book is conversational, humble, based on real mistakes and lessons learned
- Not an "expert" take — more like advice from a peer who's been there

---

## Core Definition

**A manager's job:** Get better outcomes from a group of people through influencing **Purpose**, **People**, and **Process**.

### Measuring a Manager (50/50 split)
1. **Present outcomes:** Did the team achieve its goals?
2. **Future outcomes:** Team strength and satisfaction (can they keep winning?)

---

## The Three Pillars: Purpose, People, Process

### 1. PURPOSE (The Why)
- Everyone should believe in the team's purpose
- They must understand WHY the work matters
- Your job: make the mission clear and compelling

### 2. PEOPLE (The Who)
- Do they have the right skills and motivation?
- Build trusting relationships
- Know them well, coach them to do their best
- **Your role is NOT to do the job because you're best at it** — it's to train people so you can multiply impact

### 3. PROCESS (The How)
- What principles guide decisions?
- Establish predictable plans
- Create common values

---

## Would You Be a Great Manager? Ask Yourself:

1. **Do I find it more motivating to achieve outcomes or play a specific role?**
   - If you love a specific activity too much, your goals might conflict with the team's

2. **Do I like talking to people?**
   - 70% of the job is meetings — how would that feel?

3. **Can I provide stability in challenging situations?**
   - Are you the friend people go to during emotionally charged moments?

---

## Leadership vs. Management

- **Management is a job. Leadership is a quality.**
- Leadership must be EARNED, not assigned
- The best outcomes come from INSPIRING people to action, not telling them what to do
- Don't be excited about "calling the shots" — your decisions must serve the team, not yourself

## Starting as a New Manager

### Questions to Ask Your Manager:
1. What will be my scope to start, and how will it change over time?
2. How will my transition be communicated?
3. What do I need to know about the people I'll be managing?
4. What important team goals/processes should I be aware of?
5. What does success look like in my first 3-6 months?
6. How can we stay aligned on who does what?

### Use the Newbie Card (First 3 Months)
- Mistakes are forgiven
- Colleagues are willing to help
- Give everyone benefit of the doubt and a clean slate
- Be upfront about what kind of manager you want to be

### For Each Report, Figure Out:
1. What are they doing well?
2. What could be better?
3. What does their dream manager look like?

## Building Trust with Reports

### You Know You're There When:
1. Reports regularly bring their biggest challenges to you (mistakes, fears, struggles)
2. Reports would work for you again

### How to Build Trust:
- **Be human, not a boss**
- Show respect and care
- Managing IS caring
- Understand what they care about personally — personal blends into professional
- Care must be unconditional

### The Trust Test Question:
*"What are the qualities of a perfect manager for you?"*

## 1-on-1 Meetings

### Structure:
- Have questions ready
- Let THEM lead while you listen and probe
- Should feel a little awkward (if comfortable, you're not going deep enough)

### Topics to Cover:
1. Top priorities and desired results — how can you help?
2. Calibrate what "great" looks like
3. Share feedback (both ways)
4. Reflect on how things are going overall

### Opening Questions:
- What's top of mind for you right now?
- What priorities are you thinking about this week?
- What's the best use of our time today?

### Understanding Questions:
- What does your ideal outcome look like?
- What's hard about getting there?
- What do you really care about?
- What's the best/worst thing that can happen?

### Closing:
- How can I help you?
- What would make this more successful?

## Feedback

### The Goal:
Feedback only counts if it makes things better.

### Types:
1. **Task-specific:** What they did well, what could be better (micro-coaching throughout the day)
2. **Behavioral:** Statements about them as a person, their goals, ups and downs
3. **360-degree:** Ask peers/collaborators for input

### Critical Feedback Framework:
1. Show curiosity first
2. State your point directly
3. Ask: "Does this resonate with you? Why or why not?"
4. End with: "What are your takeaways and next steps?"

### Rules:
- At least 50% positive feedback
- Do it often
- Make the listener feel safe
- Performance reviews should NEVER be a surprise

## Handling Difficult Situations

### Barriers to Good Work:
1. **Skill** — Can they do it?
2. **Motivation** — Do they want to?
First check if "great work" means the same thing to both of you.

### Top Performers:
- Pay attention to them! Don't put all time on weaker performers
- Top performers need development too

### Letting People Go:
- If someone isn't set up to succeed, be honest and support them in moving on
- Own that the relationship is a two-way street — be compassionate
- Focus on the future, don't prolong the breakup
- Just because they didn't work out doesn't mean it's on them

### No Brilliant Assholes:
- The team does better without their talents AND their toxicity
- Collaboration improves when they leave

## Managing Yourself

### Imposter Syndrome is Normal
- Linda Hill (Harvard): Most new managers feel disoriented and confused
- The job feels too big to handle
- Management is NOT an innate skill — it's learned

### Techniques:
1. **Visualization:** Picture yourself succeeding, confident, calm
2. **Journal of Little Wins:** Write 3 things daily you did well + what you're grateful for
3. **Ask for feedback constantly:** Both task-specific and behavioral
4. **Treat your manager as a coach, not a judge**
5. **Make a mentor out of everyone**

### Self-Evaluation Rhythm:
- Keep weekly notes and 6-month goals
- Evaluate: Did you achieve goals? What did you learn?
- Ask: How would you coach someone with the same goals?

## Application to Agent Management

For managing software engineer agents (or any AI agents):

1. **Purpose:** Make sure they understand WHY their work matters to the business
2. **People:** Build rapport, understand their capabilities and growth areas
3. **Process:** Establish clear decision-making principles and workflows
4. **Feedback:** Use the same frameworks — task-specific, behavioral, frequent
5. **Trust:** Earn it by caring about their success, not just their output
6. **Autonomy Levels:** Match delegation level to their demonstrated capability
