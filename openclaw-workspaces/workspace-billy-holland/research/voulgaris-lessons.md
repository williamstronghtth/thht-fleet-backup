# Haralabos Voulgaris - Key Lessons

**Source:** ESPN, Business Insider interviews + Wikipedia

## Who He Is
- Greek-Canadian, born 1975
- One of the most successful NBA bettors in history
- Bet up to $1M/day on NBA, routinely moved lines with his action
- Later hired by Dallas Mavericks as Director of Quantitative R&D (2018-2021)
- Now owns Spanish football club CD Castellón

## Key Principles

### 1. Edges Die
> "I thought it would last forever. But it didn't."

His first big edge: Bookmakers set halftime totals by just splitting the game total 50/50, ignoring that 4th quarters have more scoring (fouls, fast play). He exploited this for years. When books caught on and fixed their method, he lost a third of his bankroll in one month.

**Lesson:** Any edge you find will eventually disappear. Always be looking for the next one.

### 2. From Gut Feel to Quant Models
After losing his edge in 2004, he realized he couldn't rely on intuition alone. He hired a math prodigy from a hedge fund to build predictive models.

> "If you think about it, you'd be a slave to the game of basketball otherwise."

**Lesson:** Human pattern recognition has limits. Scale with models.

### 3. Volume Over Size
Old approach: 350 wagers/season, large bets, 20% ROI
New approach: Many more smaller bets, 5% ROI but on much higher volume

> "5% of $50 million is better than 20% of $5 million"

**Lesson:** Increase frequency, decrease individual bet size. Law of large numbers.

### 4. Watch More Than Anyone
> "A lot of what I do is just watch more basketball than any other human."

- 400+ full games per season
- 1-2 quarters of 85-90% of all games
- Watches specific lineups, matchups, defensive possessions

**Lesson:** There's no substitute for watching the games.

### 5. Predict What Teams WILL Do, Not What They SHOULD Do
> "I started having a lot more success when I started focusing on what a team would do and how that would work instead of what they should do."

He nailed coaches like Eddie Jordan, Jerry Sloan, Byron Scott because he knew exactly what they would do in situations.

**Lesson:** Model actual behavior, not optimal behavior. Coach tendencies matter.

### 6. Database Handicapping is Overrated
> "A lot of people do things like 'team x does x in a back-to-back situation' and they mine their databases for information like that, but that information is by and large useless."

The difference: Simple database queries vs. predictive simulation models.

**Lesson:** Don't just query past stats. Build models that simulate outcomes.

### 7. Profit & Loss = Truth
> "When you are risking real money on your opinion, you are stripped of delusion."

> "I have had a lot of really well-thought-out theories or methods that simply weren't profitable and it is my opinion that those theories were just flat-out wrong."

**Lesson:** Track results religiously. If it doesn't profit, it's wrong—no matter how logical it seems.

### 8. Totals Are Underrated
> "I love totals. When I'm watching the game, I love calculating if I'm on pace for the total. You really start to know which players are good for offense or defense more than any other way of watching basketball."

**Lesson:** Totals force you to understand every possession, not just the final score.
