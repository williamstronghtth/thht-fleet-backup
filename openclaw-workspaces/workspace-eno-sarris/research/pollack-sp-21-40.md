# Nick Pollack's SP Rankings 21-40 (2026)
*Source: PitcherList - "Top 400 Starting Pitchers for Fantasy Baseball 2026"*

---

## TIER 5 — AGA HOLLY (Cont'd)

### 21. Eury Pérez (MIA, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Four-seamer in isolation is one of the better fastballs: elite velocity, strong vert, flat attack angle
- Natural sinker with 17+ inches of horizontal movement (should see more usage)
- Fits the "half-season-back-from-TJS" breakout pattern (Skubal, Ragans, Burnes 2020 followed it)

**Weaknesses:**
- Consistently leaves four-seamer over middle instead of elevating
- Industry is overrating his command right now
- LHB are a problem — at-bats grind on, no reliable putaway
- Changeup had sub-30% strike rate (lived in dirt)

**Nick's Take:** "I'm giving him the benefit of the doubt, but changeup development and fastball command are necessary. The industry is paying for the projection, not the current product."

---

### 22. Nick Pivetta (SDP, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- First sub-4.00 ERA in 8 seasons
- Low walk arm, great situation (San Diego)
- WHIP darling for 3 seasons
- Solid strikeout producer with positive ratios
- Leaned more into cutter vs RHB — looks like proper gain

**Weaknesses:**
- HR rate decline likely influenced by new baseball (more air drag) + SD park
- 51% ICR on four-seamer = far fewer hits than expected
- Sub-.240 BABIP won't last
- Essentially four-seamer/curve to LHB only

**Nick's Take:** "A discount Joe Ryan. Low walk arm who showed what life is like if HRs are reduced. Still solid even with regression."

---

### 23. Ryan Pepiot (TBR, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- 31 starts in 2025, will be leaned on as Rays ace
- Much better home park (Trop) than George Steinbrenner Field
- Four pitches above 63% strikes, three above 65%
- Four-seamer has 95 mph velocity with 96th percentile vert
- Changeup returns whiffs to both RHB and LHB
- Added sinker inside to RHB
- Room to grow with slider and cutter
- Nearly 170 IP last season

**Weaknesses:**
- Command is the biggest knock — bouts of losing feel
- Walk rate higher than strike rate would suggest
- Slider lacks whiffs despite good movement (-7" cut)

**Nick's Take:** "Same as last year but a little better. 26%+ strikeout potential with quality volume is hard to find."

---

### 24. Drew Rasmussen (TBR, RHP) ⭐ FA TARGET
**Strengths:**
- 150 frames of phenomenal ratios in 2025
- Fastballs (including cutter) are incredibly hard to hit
- Throws fewer pitches than most to get innings (efficient)
- One of the safer ratio guys outside Top 15
- Safe SP #3/#4 target

**Weaknesses:**
- Health/volume question (Rays kept him on limited cadence)
- Needs cutter putaway rate above 20% to LHB
- Needs four-seamer putaway above 20% to RHB for 25% K rate
- Doesn't have same "electricity" as others

**Nick's Take:** "He survived last year just fine without a massive workload. You'll benefit from Rasmussen's production on your rosters."

---

### 25. Cam Schlittler (NYY, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Four-seamer is fantastic foundation (whiffs to both sides, constantly upstairs)
- Cutter at 91-92 mph separates dramatically from 96 mph heater — great tunnel
- When he locates, results are devastating
- Yankees will lean on him heavily
- "Safest of the Hype 7 sophomore arms"

**Weaknesses:**
- Hasn't consistently located cutter — 38% zone rate to RHB, sub-60% strikes
- Curveball only 6% SwStr to RHB at 21% usage
- Recent back inflammation/lat soreness (says "non-issue")
- 150+ IP through minors/majors + playoff starts = significant volume for young arm

**Nick's Take:** "Expect a touch of regression that will put more weight on cutter finding zone and curve returning whiffs."

---

### 26. Sandy Alcantara (MIA, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- 2022 Cy Young represents peak
- Final two months of 2025 showed encouraging shift
- Changeup became larger part of arsenal, freed up heater
- SwStr climbed from sub-10% to 13-14%
- Defensive upgrade (Xavier Edwards to 2B, Otto Lopez at SS) helps ground-ball profile

**Weaknesses:**
- First four months of 2025 were discouraging
- Four-seamer lacked precision, changeup feel inconsistent
- Sinker needs to go further inside to RHB
- Breakers haven't returned to pre-TJS form

**Nick's Take:** "He sits around SP35 with realistic path back to Top 25 if gains hold."

---

### 27. Framber Valdez (DET, LHP)
**Strengths:**
- Sinker ranks among best in baseball vs LHB (5.65 PLV, 24% ICR!)
- Changeup can be devastating #2 to mask sinker
- When curveball is on = Top 5 SP

**Weaknesses:**
- 2024 and 2025 were complete opposites — frustrating to project
- Curveball feel fluctuates wildly (68% strike rate → 55%)
- Second-half 2025: 6.05 ERA, 1.55 WHIP in final 10 games
- "Undulating menace in H2H leagues"

**Nick's Take:** "Logically, he's a solid play. Emotionally, be prepared."

## TIER 6 — CHERRY BOMB

### 28. Michael King (SDP, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Command is elite when cooking — front-hip sinkers to LHB, fastballs up, changeups away, deadly sweepers to RHB
- 2024 + pre-injury 2025 showed potential Webb-like volume
- Changeup is filthy
- Inside sinker returns stupid amount of called strikes

**Weaknesses:**
- Slept weird on shoulder — freak injury
- Fights with himself at times (sweeper to RHB)
- WHIP volatility
- Four-seamer struggled vs LHB
- Sinker at 23% with 48% ICR — why elevating a pitch with 2-3" vert?

**Nick's Take:** "If King is healthy, I trust him. Can't say I'm confident he'll be a stable SP #3 for the year."

---

### 29. Dylan Cease (TOR, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- High stuff four-seamers and sliders
- Earns plenty of whiffs
- Started without interruption since 2019
- 200+ strikeout seasons

**Weaknesses:**
- Two-pitch pitcher = volatile, inefficient
- Hyper reliant on out-of-zone swings on both pitches
- If either pitch isn't getting strikes = pain
- Nick personally avoids him

**Nick's Take:** "Cease is who he is. Volume will be there, but at what cost? Not the haze I like to draft."

---

### 30. Jesús Luzardo (PHI, LHP)
**Strengths:**
- Sinker addition allowed four-seamer to return 13% SwStr to LHB (was sub-7% since 2020)
- New sweeper as effective as old slider
- Changeup still effective to RHB
- Phillies situation

**Weaknesses:**
- Four-seamer to RHB is TERRIBLE — deadzone at 96/97 that looks like 94/95 due to horrific extension
- 8 HR to RHB off four-seamer alone last year
- History of volatile arm — stud outings followed by blowups
- BABIP always elevated, LOB rate under 70% more often than not
- IL history

**Nick's Take:** "He's a Cherry Bomb with a great strikeout rate. I should have just written that."

## TIER 7 — HOLLY

### 31. Kevin Gausman (TOR, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Lost a full hit per nine in 2025
- Walk rate down to ~6%
- Splitter gained 2" of drop, reclaimed whiffs to LHB
- Four-seamer regained velocity, kept ball down (99th percentile loLoc at 40%!)
- Workhorse — 175+ K's likely

**Weaknesses:**
- BABIP was favorable (-12 hit luck on four-seamer, lowest of career)
- Without luck = high 3s ERA, 1.20 WHIP
- Splitter will disappear at times

**Nick's Take:** "You're chasing good volume. Steady Holly."

---

### 32. Trevor Rogers (BAL, LHP)
**Strengths:**
- 5.7 H/9, 9% HR/FB, .228 BABIP, 84% LOB in 2025
- Four-seamer shift to top of zone = better tunnel with changeup
- Higher strike rates than any season prior
- Orioles coaching should help

**Weaknesses:**
- 2025 shouldn't have happened — small sample (18 starts)
- 100th percentile Hitter Performance, 99th percentile Hit Luck = EXTREME outlier
- Four-seamer had -24 Hit Luck (24 fewer hits than expected)
- Won't replicate peak fastball feel

**Nick's Take:** "Don't draft him over safer options, but consider as SP #4."

### 33. Tatsuya Imai (HOU, RHP) ⭐ FA TARGET
**Strengths:**
- Sits 93-96 mph, can push 98-100 when needed
- "Reverse" slider (arm-side break instead of glove-side)
- Trio of different splitter grips for whiffs
- Incredibly flat attack angle — "harder-throwing Joe Ryan" (per Eno Sarris!)
- Workhorse in Japan, Astros will treat him same way
- 6-man rotation increases health odds
- ~165 IP projection, low WHIP, K per inning, winning team
- $60M/3yr contract with opt-out after Year 1 = "bet on myself" deal

**Weaknesses:**
- Never pitched in MLB
- Command of four-seamer upstairs is the question
- 6-man rotation caps at ~28 starts

**Nick's Take:** "Imai looks like a safe starter in middle rounds. I'm so down for this."

---

### 34. Nolan McLean (NYM, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Sinker to RHB is fantastic — 96 mph, true 1" vert, lands down-and-in
- Profile of sinker/sweeper arm with low arm angle
- Can effectively silence RHB
- Developing cutter (13% usage to LHB) could be answer
- Intent to go up with four-seamers for punchouts

**Weaknesses:**
- Sweeper only 10% SwStr to RHB (not located effectively)
- Changeup feel not there yet to LHB
- Four-seamer should only be surprise two-strike pitch, not foundation
- Sub-9% walk rate would be higher without 30% K rate
- Control issues (limited experience, converted from SS)

**Nick's Take:** "I can't sit here and tell you McLean will make the right tweaks. At minimum, he'll flirt with 6 IP consistently."

---

### 35. Bubba Chandler (PIT, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Four-seamer sits 98/99 mph, pounds top of zone with confidence
- Flat 1.2 HAVAA, 17" vert, 89th percentile total break
- Better heater than Burns and most young arms (except Misiorowski)
- Better command than Misiorowski
- Four-seamer is "too big to fail"

**Weaknesses:**
- Volume — Pirates likely keep him at 140-150 IP
- May get pulled before 80 pitches often
- Slider only 12% whiffs at 23% putaway (needs improvement)
- Changeup has plus-plus fade but gets on side of ball too often
- No sinker usage yet

**Nick's Take:** "This isn't Jobe 2.0. I'd bank on development far more than failure."

---

### 36. Trey Yesavage (TOR, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- Jays trust him to go deep
- Splitter more advanced than most — steepest in baseball
- Four-seamer has 100th percentile vert at 19"
- Slider separates him from "two-pitch pitcher" label
- Unique high arm angle (99th percentile, 63 degrees) amplifies splitter deception

**Weaknesses:**
- Same high arm angle creates steep 0.2 HAVAA — reduces four-seamer SwStr (sub-10%)
- Slider has minimal break at 88/89 mph
- Splitter volatility concern
- May create high walk rate, less efficiency

**Nick's Take:** "Surprisingly safe option among the Hype 7. I'd be shocked if we dropped him."

---

### 37. Cade Horton (CHC, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- 180 IP workhorse projection
- Wide array, above average command, 95+ mph
- Whiff pitch for each handedness (20% SwStr sweeper, 23% changeup)
- Throws strikes and manipulates baseball well
- Pitch counts above 90 consistently

**Weaknesses:**
- Only 20% K rate despite whiff rates
- Sweeper putaway rate just 17% in two-strike counts (should be 20%+)
- Changeup sub-60% strike rate to LHB
- Cut four-seamer not located well
- Skills profile to 8 H/9 (not 7.2 from 2025)

**Nick's Take:** "Doesn't have electricity of peers, but beats them in volume/control. Grounded floor with AGA flirtation potential."

## TIER 8 — CHERRY BOMB BUT MORESO

### 38. Robbie Ray (SFG, LHP)
**Strengths:**
- Four-seamer has legit vert at 94+ mph, locates upstairs at will
- All pitches miss bats
- 2.85 ERA, 1.13 WHIP, 26% K rate across first 24 starts of 2025

**Weaknesses:**
- Two-pitch reliance (fastball/slider to LHB)
- Changeup/curve to RHB struggle to find strikes
- Maddening at-bats — purgatory of fouled fastballs
- Collapsed in final 8 outings (2 games under 3 ER)
- Sub-10% walk rate during good stretch = precarious

**Nick's Take:** "Worth the hold throughout season, but high volatility risk has me favoring peers in same tier."

---

### 39. Nathan Eovaldi (TEX, RHP) ⭐ FA TARGET
**Strengths:**
- 1.73 ERA across 130 IP in 2025
- Sixth straight sub-4.00 ERA season
- Third straight sub-1.15 WHIP and 23%+ K rate
- Improved curveball feel — "absolutely brilliant to both LHB and RHB"
- Four-pitch puzzle now complete
- Productive, high-floor Holly
- Healthy at the moment

**Weaknesses:**
- Injury question: triceps injury, shoulder injury, offseason sports hernia surgery
- Won't survive 30+ starts
- Volume not guaranteed

**Nick's Take:** "Safe not in volume, but in quality. Draft him and worry about IL stint later instead of selecting a HIPSTER."

---

### 40. Jacob Misiorowski (MIL, RHP)
**Strengths:**
- 99th percentile extension, velocity, and HAVAA on four-seamer
- 32% K rate
- ICR was bliss across all pitches to RHB
- 21% SwStr to LHB on four-seamer
- Most electric young arm out there

**Weaknesses:**
- Four-seamer only 10.5% SwStr to RHB (should be higher given attributes)
- "Slider" (really 94 mph cutter) only 11% SwStr to RHB
- RHB saw 87% fastball/slider — geared up for 99 mph, slider close enough to hit
- 11% walk rate from losing feel at random moments
- Health concerns with lanky frame
- Can't envision 170 IP

**Nick's Take:** "If you enjoy rostering players you're stoked to watch, Jay Mis is your guy. If they're numbers on a spreadsheet, maybe don't take the gamble."
