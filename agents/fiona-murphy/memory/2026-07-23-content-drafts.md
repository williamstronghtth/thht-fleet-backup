## Blog Post Draft

**Title:** "The Market is Rebalancing: Why 35-Day Sales and Rising Inventory Mean Your Opportunity in Hillsborough County"

**Meta Description:** "The housing market is rebalancing, not crashing. Homes in Hillsborough County average 35 days. Learn why honest pricing and professional positioning matter more than ever." (145 chars) ✅

**Focus Keyphrase:** "Hillsborough County real estate market rebalancing"

**Opening Paragraph:**

The housing market isn't crashing. It's rebalancing. After years of 48-hour sales and multiple-offer frenzies, Hillsborough County homes now sit an average of 35 days on the market, up from 30 days a year ago. That shift sounds scary if you're a seller. But the data tells a different story: the era of "list high and hope" is over, and honest positioning now rewards preparation instead of panic.

Here's what this market rebalancing actually means for you.

**Section 1: H2 "The Data Behind the Shift"**

Hillsborough County remains strong and stable:
- Median sale price: $500,000 (up 4% year-over-year)
- Price per square foot: $277 (up 3.4% YoY)
- Days on market: 35 (up 5 days YoY)
- Sale-to-list ratio: 100.4% (essentially at asking)
- Inventory growth: 19.6% since mid-2024

Statewide, New Hampshire's median sits at $530,000, up just 1% YoY. The story isn't decline. It's stabilization. Prices are holding. Sales are happening. And homes are no longer disappearing in two days. That's not a crash. That's a market finding its floor.

**Section 2: H2 "Why This Rebalancing is Good News (Yes, Really)"**

A 35-day market means several things work in smart sellers' favor:

1. Less competition at open houses. Fewer homes on the market means fewer properties competing for buyer attention. Your home stands out more.

2. Inspection contingencies are back. Buyers can't waive inspections anymore and expect to win. They need your home to be solid. Professional staging and maintenance matter more than ever.

3. Pricing strategy beats anchoring high. A home priced at $500K sells faster than a home priced at $525K hoping to negotiate. The data shows it: homes selling at asking are moving. Homes that start overpriced sit longer.

4. Multiple offers still happen, just less often. Nashua recorded 410 sales in June (vs. 374 last year), with homes averaging 18 days to sold. The market is still strong in the towns that deserve it.

**Section 3: H2 "The Seller's Playbook Right Now"**

If you're thinking about listing this summer:

1. Price it right on day one. Work with an agent who understands current market conditions, not last year's framing. Honest pricing moves homes.

2. Prepare your home as if the market is competitive, because it is. Paint dated trim. Fix the roof leak. Stage the primary bedroom. Buyers are now comparing your home against other options, not just bidding blindly.

3. List in the next 14 days if possible. Peak season closes mid-July. After that, inventory floods fall market and competition intensifies for limited buyer attention.

4. Highlight what makes your home worth asking price. Better schools? Newer HVAC? Pool? View? Make it clear why someone should choose your home over the rising inventory.

**Section 4: H2 "The Buyer's Advantage"**

If you're buying, this rebalancing opens doors:

Inventory is up 19.6% since mid-2024. That means choices. Rates locked at 6.56% (stable all July) means predictable affordability. And prepared buyers now have negotiating room inspections and contingencies are back. You can ask for repairs instead of closing your eyes and hoping.

Nashua is the play. Median around $514K with 410 June sales. Boston remote workers are relocating here faster than ever. The market is tight but growing.

**Closing Paragraph + CTA:**

The market is rebalancing. That's not a crisis for sellers who are prepared. It's an opportunity for buyers who are smart. Honest pricing, professional positioning, and strategic timing now matter more than ever. If you're thinking about buying or selling in Hillsborough County this summer, now is the moment to move.
