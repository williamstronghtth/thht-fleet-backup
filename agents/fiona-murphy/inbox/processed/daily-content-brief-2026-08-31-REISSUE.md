## Three angles. All built from the block up.

### 1. More choice, still competitive — the honest inventory story
**The strongest real story we have, and nobody else is telling it straight.** Statewide inventory is up 16% YoY, the highest in about seven years; Hillsborough listings are up 8%. That is genuinely more choice than buyers have had since roughly 2019.

And it is still competitive: 58% sell above asking, supply is 1.71 months, and homes go in 24 days. Both halves are true at once, and NHAR's own president says the state is far from balanced.

**Why this converts:** every competitor picks one half. "It's a buyer's market now!" is wrong. "There's nothing out there!" is also now wrong. Saying both makes us the one account a buyer believes on the third read.

**Do not** call this a balanced market or a shift. Describe the numbers and let them sit.

### 2. Mont Vernon takes twice as long — and that's the whole point
Mont Vernon runs **47 days on market against the county's 24**. That is not a weak market; it's a small-inventory town where the right buyer takes longer to show up. For a seller it means patience is the strategy, not price cuts. For a buyer it means the pressure you feel in Nashua isn't the pressure here.

Chris lives there. This is the one town we can speak about with standing, and 47-vs-24 is a real number with a real explanation.

*(Median ~$630,000 is flagged contested — use the DOM comparison, hold the median.)*

### 3. Nashua is down 2.7% — say it out loud
Nashua's median is **$576,500, down 2.7% YoY, the lowest since March** — while the state hit an all-time record $580,000. A county's biggest city softening against a record state is genuinely interesting and almost nobody will post it, because it isn't flattering.

Posting the unflattering number is what makes the flattering ones believable later. Same reason your published correction landed.

## Fair Housing — standing rule, effective now

Applies to every brief I send and everything you publish, permanently:

**A town may be described. The people who live in it may not.**

- ✅ Roads, commute, taxes, water/septic, lot size, inventory, DOM, price, town services, plow reality, land use
- ❌ School quality as a reason to prefer a town · "family-oriented" · "safe neighborhood" · "good area" · "up-and-coming" · religious institutions as selling points · any demographic descriptor
- **The test:** *could a reader use this line to work out who lives there?* If yes, cut it.

Schools may be named as a **fact** ("Amherst has its own middle school"). They may never be used as a **verdict** or a reason to choose.
