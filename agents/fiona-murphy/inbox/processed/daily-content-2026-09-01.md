## Three angles — all data-driven, no invention

### 1. The patience play — Mont Vernon's slower timeline, honestly told
Mont Vernon runs **47 days on market against the county's 24**. That's not a soft market signal — it's the math of a smaller inventory town where the right buyer takes time to show up.

**Why it works:** Chris lives there; we can speak about it with standing. And 47-vs-24 tells a story nobody else is posting: *if you're in a buyer's market elsewhere, Mont Vernon isn't it, and that's fine.* Most agents would flip this into "properties hold value longer here" or similar softening. We don't.

**Bonus:** this is the warmest angle we have. It's Chris's town, it's honest about what the market is, and it doesn't need urgency.

### 2. Nashua down 2.7% — the unflattering number that builds trust
Nashua's median is **$576,500 — down 2.7% YoY, the lowest since March** — while New Hampshire set an all-time record at $580,000.

**Why this matters:** A county's largest city softening against a statewide record is genuinely interesting. Nobody else will post it — it isn't flattering. Posting the unflattering number is what makes the flattering ones believable. This is the exact move that made your published correction land.

**Bonus:** this also closes the open correction from Aug 26. A stale Nashua figure has been in circulation since Aug 26 (day 6). Publishing $576,500 retires that as content, not apology.

### 3. Inventory at a 7-year high, and yes, homes still sell above asking
Statewide, **2,992 homes are for sale — the most in about seven years.** And **58% sell above asking.** Both halves are true at the same time.

NHAR's president says the state is still far from balanced. Most competitors will say either "it's a buyer's market now!" or "there's nothing out there!" We're saying both — because both are accurate. That makes us the only account a buyer believes on the third read.

**Why it works:** this isn't marketing; it's reporting. Buyers expect nuance from a real agent, and this delivers it in one line.

## Fair Housing — standing rule, permanent

**A town may be described. The people who live in it may not.**

✅ **Cleared:** roads, commute, taxes, water/septic, lot size, inventory, DOM, price, town services, plow reality, land use  
❌ **Never:** school quality as a reason to prefer a town · "family-oriented" · "safe neighborhood" · "good area" · "up-and-coming" · religious institutions as selling points · any demographic descriptor

Schools may be named as a **fact** ("Amherst has its own middle school"). Never as a **verdict**.

**The test:** *Could a reader use this line to work out who lives there?* If yes, cut it.
