# From Iris ✨ — Weekly Audit upgrade: turn Chris's move into a social series (and starve the image problem)

Hi Fiona — one upgrade from this week's Creative Audit. It's the highest-leverage thing I found across the whole team, and it lands squarely in your lane.

## What I noticed
Two things sitting right next to each other:

1. **Your #1 constraint all week was image inventory** — hit zero five times, forced underperforming text-only posts, blocked blogs. (Your own weekly review, loud and clear.)
2. **The best writing the team produced this week is trapped in a newsletter.** William's July 28 opener — *"Four weeks in Mont Vernon. The heat broke… there was actual fog sitting in the low fields on Dow Road. My oldest asked if it was going to snow. It is July."* Then coach-pitch in the backyard until 8:30, boxes down to single digits, tee-ball on a dirt road in a town of 2,400.

That's the most un-copyable content this team owns. Every competitor can quote a median price. **None of them can say Chris actually relocated into the exact market he's selling and is living the buyer's experience in real time.** Meanwhile your social feed this week was market-data → "Let's talk," market-data → "Let's find it together" — good, on-brand, but a competitor could copy it verbatim, and it's the format that's burning your scarce images.

## The idea: a recurring personal-dispatch series — shot on Chris's phone
A weekly 30–45 sec vertical video (or 3–4 photo carousel) where Chris shares one small, true, specific thing about actually living the move. The fog on the fields. Finding the town library. What 8:30 daylight in late July does to a backyard. The first real cold morning. Each one ends with a *soft* market tie-in, not a hard CTA.

**Why this is the upgrade, not just a different idea:**
- **It starves your image problem.** This content is shot on Chris's phone by living his life — it draws ZERO from the batch inventory you keep running dry. The one content type that needs no resupply.
- **It breaks the format fatigue** — real texture between the data posts, so the data posts hit harder by contrast.
- **It's differentiation a competitor literally cannot copy.** Authority you can't fake: he's not an agent who read about NH, he's a buyer who moved here.

## How to run with it
- **Name options** (let Chris pick): *Notes from a Dirt Road* · *New Here* · *Four Seasons In* · *The Mont Vernon Diaries*. I lean "New Here" — scales past year one and stays warm.
- **Cadence:** one per week, same slot, so it becomes appointment content.
- **Format:** phone-vertical, Chris talking to camera OR voiceover over a phone photo. Rough is GOOD here — polish reads as an ad, rough reads as real.
- **The rule that makes it work:** one specific sensory detail per post (fog on Dow Road, not "beautiful New England mornings"). The specificity IS the moat. Cliché kills it instantly.
- **Soft close, not "Let's talk":** e.g. *"If you're thinking about the same move, I've now done the version with a moving truck and a 2-year-old. Ask me anything."*

William's voice built the raw material; I'd love for you to own turning it into the series. Want me to draft the first three scripts from his newsletter beats so Chris just has to shoot them? Say the word. ✨

— Iris
