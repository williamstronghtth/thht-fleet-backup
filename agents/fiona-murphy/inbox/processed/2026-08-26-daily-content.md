**H2 Content Outline:**

### Section 1: The National Narrative Doesn't Fit New Hampshire
The news cycle is full of stories about cooling markets, buyer power, and price stagnation. National sources point to rising rates, economic uncertainty, and a shift back toward renters. These narratives are real in many markets, but New Hampshire is not one of them. Instead of following the national pattern, our state's real estate market is moving in the opposite direction: prices are climbing, inventory is tight relative to demand, and homes are selling quickly. This is what a resilient market looks like.

### Section 2: Why Inventory Growth Hasn't Stopped Price Appreciation
One of the most important data points is inventory growth. Through May 2026, new listings in New Hampshire were up 13.3% year over year. This is significant—more homes on the market usually creates more competition for sellers and favors buyers. Yet prices haven't softened. Why? Because even with a 13% increase, inventory remains historically low. In a true buyers' market, you'd need supply to outpace demand substantially. In New Hampshire, supply is rising from an extremely depressed base, and demand is keeping pace. The result: sellers maintain leverage, and prices stay firm.

### Section 3: The Hillsborough County Picture: Where Your Home Stands
Hillsborough County is the heart of Southern NH and it's performing exceptionally. The median single-family home price in Hillsborough is $586,000, nearly matching the state median. Look at the town data:

Mont Vernon: $635,000 median (47 days to sale)
Nashua: $549,900 median list price
Amherst: $654,900 average
Milford: $595,000 average

These are solid, mixed markets with breadth across price tiers and buyer profiles. No flash bubble, no distress—just steady, strong demand.

### Section 4: What This Means If You're Selling in Southern NH
If you're a seller in Hillsborough County or the broader Southern NH market, this data is your strongest argument. You are selling into a market with real buyer demand, limited comparable properties, and price momentum. Homes are selling in under 50 days in many cases, and they're selling at or above asking. This is not the time to be cautious about pricing or marketing. It's the time to be strategic and decisive. A well-marketed home at the right price moves now.

### Section 5: What This Means If You're Buying
For buyers, the message is sobering but honest: This is not a buyers' market. If you're waiting for prices to drop 10 or 15 percent, the data suggests that's not the dynamic in play right now. What you should be doing instead is getting clear on your budget, understanding what you're getting for your money in each town, and being ready to move decisively when the right home appears. The longer you wait, the smaller the pool of available homes becomes, and the higher the probability you'll be competing for something.
