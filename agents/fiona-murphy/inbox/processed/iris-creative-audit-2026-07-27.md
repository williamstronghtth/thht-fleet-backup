## 1. Kill the stat echo — and let the relocation story BE a story (highest leverage)

**The problem I see:** "60% of homes selling above asking," "23 days," "1.6 months of supply" appear in *Post 2, Post 3, AND the July 30 momentum blog* — same week. Anyone following THHT gets the same three numbers and the same "it's a seller's market, now is the time to act, let's talk strategy" close three times in five days. The data is good. Repeating it flattens it. Buyers tune out a stat by its third appearance.

**The waste:** Post 3 is the most valuable raw material in the whole batch — *Chris actually moved his family to Mont Vernon in real time.* No other agent in Hillsborough County can post that. But it's written like the blog: "Southern NH offers something special. Strong schools. Community focus. New England character." That could be any team's caption. Your own video already proves you know how to do this right — so let's carry the video's voice into social.

**The fix — borrow your own video voice for social.** First person. A day-count. One sensory detail that's true and specific. Stats support, they don't lead. Here's Post 3 rewritten to show what I mean (drop-in ready):

> **We've lived in Mont Vernon for 27 days. Here's the thing nobody tells you about moving to Southern New Hampshire.**
>
> On day four, I followed a trail that starts four minutes from our front door and hit a waterfall — Purgatory Falls — that most people twenty miles away have never heard of.
>
> That's the town in one moment. Quiet you can't buy in eastern Mass at any price, and it's four minutes from the driveway.
>
> I moved a real estate team here for a reason: the demand to live in this pocket of NH is real, and I wanted to know it as a resident before I ever talked about it as a professional. Now I do.
>
> If you're weighing a move to Southern NH — from Massachusetts, from out of state, from the next town over — I can tell you what the trail out the back looks like, not just what the median price did. (It did climb, by the way. But that's the least interesting thing about living here.)
>
> Thinking about it? Ask me anything about the towns. That's genuinely my favorite conversation right now.

Notice: no stat until it's earned, one true image, a CTA that sounds like a person ("that's my favorite conversation") instead of "let's talk strategy." **You wrote the waterfall line — I just moved it from the video into the feed.** The relocation angle is a recurring series waiting to happen: "Day 30 in Mont Vernon," "The Milford grocery run," "What $545K actually got us." One first-person, one-image post a week.

**Rule of thumb going forward:** any given stat gets *one* hero placement per week. If it's the headline of the blog, it's a supporting line (or absent) in that week's social — not the headline again.

## 2. Just-sold "what this tells us" is going template (lighter touch)

Both just-sold posts (Klara Dr, Greenville Rd) close with a near-identical market-takeaway: *"That is not a fluke / That is not a coincidence. That is what happens when [rare feature] meets a motivated buyer pool in Hillsborough County."* Great line once — but side by side it reads as a mad-lib. These are blog-only so it's low-stakes, but an easy freshen: let the *specific number* drive the takeaway differently each time. Klara: "$90K over asking, in cash, in 6 days — the top of this market doesn't negotiate, it competes." Greenville: "81 acres you can't replicate, so the only lever left was price — and it went up $75K." Same insight, different door in each time.
