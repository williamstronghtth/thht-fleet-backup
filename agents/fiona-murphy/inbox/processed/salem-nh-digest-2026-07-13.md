**Key Takeaways:**
- Salem's market is still highly competitive — homes routinely receive multiple offers and sell above list.
- Median list price dipped ~5% from June to July 2026, which may signal a brief seasonal pause rather than a softening trend.
- Inventory is slightly more available than the tightest years (2021–2023), but supply remains constrained.
- **Boston commuter demand is a structural driver.** Salem's no-income-tax, lower price point vs. Greater Boston continues to pull buyers across the MA border.
- The market is broadly balanced — buyers who are prepared and realistic on timing are finding opportunities.

## 7. Infrastructure, Development & Construction

**Bridge Street Bridge Replacement — ACTIVE / MAJOR**
Bridge Street is **closed to traffic** between Wheeler Avenue and Main Street since January 12, 2026, for a full bridge replacement. The closure is expected to last approximately **10 months**, meaning it will extend into late fall 2026. Residents and visitors should plan alternate routes; this is an active disruption to central Salem circulation.

**Route 111 / Ermer Road Roundabout — Construction Season Underway**
NHDOT has accelerated this project. A **single-lane roundabout** plus roadway and lighting improvements are under construction at the Route 111/Ermer Road intersection. The Wetlands Bureau permit was cleared in January 2026. Construction is actively advancing this summer.

**Tuscan Village — West Village District Revision**
The most significant long-term development story in Salem. A revised conceptual plan for the **West Village District** (previously approved as a Life Science District) is set for Planning Board review on **July 28, 2026**. The new proposal includes:
- 392 market-rate apartments + 72 workforce housing units (464 total)
- 120-room hotel
- Two office buildings
- Bank, quick-serve restaurant with drive-thru, and sit-down restaurant

The shift away from Life Science reflects reduced demand for lab space. This replaces an earlier 2025 concept that included 133 luxury condos, 50 workforce units, a hotel, and medical offices.

**Tuscan Village Fiscal Impact — By the Numbers (June 2026 Town Presentation)**
- Combined assessed value of Tuscan Village properties: ~$31M (2021) → **$463M+ (2025)**
- Property tax revenue from Tuscan Village in 2025: **~$9.87 million** (up ~85% since 2022)
- Tuscan Village now represents approximately **7.3% of Salem's total property tax base**
- If the site had remained the old Rockingham Park racetrack, Salem homeowners' tax bills would be an estimated **$675/year higher** on a $610K home
- Development estimated to be **~two-thirds complete** — significant additional assessed value still to come
