**Key takeaway:** Mont Vernon is a thin, high-value market. With roughly 37 transactions per year, a single agent who deeply knows the town can dominate. List prices ($799K median) run significantly above actual sale prices ($630–635K), suggesting sellers are aspirational and well-informed buyers have negotiating room.

### Inventory Dynamics

Mont Vernon's housing stock is extremely limited. The town has fewer than 900 occupied housing units, of which approximately 89.5% are owner-occupied. New listings are infrequent — typically only a handful come to market in any given month. When quality homes do appear, they attract serious buyers quickly. Active inventory in June 2026 sits at roughly 10 homes across all sources, a figure consistent with the town's small scale.

### Price Context vs. Neighboring Towns

| Town | Median/Average Home Price |
|---|---|
| Mont Vernon | ~$630,000–$670,000 (sold) |
| Amherst | ~$594,000–$595,000 |
| Milford | ~$443,000–$447,000 |
| Hillsborough County | $548,750 (March 2026) |

Mont Vernon commands a modest premium over Amherst and a substantial premium over Milford. The gap reflects Mont Vernon's rural character, larger lot sizes, lower density, and — critically — its top-ranked elementary school.

### New Construction Activity

There is limited but active new construction in town. The Forest View II subdivision features custom homes on lots of 1+ acres. Individual custom builders — typically local craftspeople building only a handful of homes per year — produce the majority of new stock. Ranch and Colonial styles on 2–5 acre lots are the dominant new-build form. Seven new construction listings are actively available as of early June 2026.

### Broader Market Context

Southern NH remains a seller's market, though it is moderating. Statewide inventory increased ~19.6% between mid-2024 and mid-2025 and that trend has continued into spring 2026. However, New Hampshire's months of supply still sits at just 1.4 months — far below the 4–6 months that defines a balanced market. Hillsborough County closed sales were up 6.6% YoY in March 2026. The market is cooling at the margin, not collapsing — buyers have more time to think, sellers still hold structural leverage.

## 2. Town Profile

### The Basics

- **Population:** ~2,607 (U.S. Census)
- **Land area:** 16.6 square miles
- **Density:** ~145 people per square mile (very rural)
- **County:** Hillsborough
- **ZIP code:** 03057
- **Metro area:** Manchester-Nashua, NH MSA

### Demographics & Income

Mont Vernon skews affluent and established. The median household income is $156,389 — nearly double the national median — and the average household income reaches $186,688. Homeownership sits at 89.5%, confirming this is a community of long-term residents who invest in their town. The median age is 43.8 years, reflecting the family-with-school-age-children and empty-nester segments that dominate the market.

### Commute & Location

Mont Vernon is strategically positioned for southern NH commuters:

- **Manchester:** ~20 miles north (~25–30 minutes)
- **Nashua:** ~18 miles east (~30 minutes)
- **Boston:** ~60 miles south (~70–90 minutes via Rte 3/I-93)
- **Concord (state capital):** ~35 miles north (~40 minutes)

Average commute time is 33.3 minutes for residents. The vast majority (98%+) drive; the town has no meaningful public transit. This is firmly drive-to-work territory — residents accept the tradeoff of rural quiet for a commute window.

New Hampshire has no state income tax and no state sales tax, which represents meaningful financial savings for households relocating from Massachusetts. This remains a top relocation driver from the Boston metro area.

### Mont Vernon Village School — A Genuine Asset

The elementary school is Mont Vernon's most frequently cited residential draw and deserves detailed treatment in any buyer conversation:

| Metric | Mont Vernon Village School | NH Statewide |
|---|---|---|
| Grades served | K–6 | — |
| Enrollment | ~234 students | — |
| Student-teacher ratio | 12:1 | ~14:1 |
| Math proficiency | 85% | 42% |
| Reading proficiency | 78% | 55% |
| GreatSchools rating | 9/10 | — |
| Niche grade | A+ | — |
| NH elementary school rank | #11 of 224 | Top 5% |

These are genuinely outstanding numbers. Math proficiency that is more than double the state average in a public school is not incidental — it reflects a community that funds, values, and participates in its school. For buyers with children in K–6, this school alone justifies the price premium over Milford.

**For grades 7–12**, Mont Vernon students attend Milford Middle School and Milford High School (SAU 39), which have solid but less exceptional ratings. This is an important detail to know and share transparently with families — the exceptional academics cover elementary years, and families with older children should tour Milford schools separately.

### Character & Community Feel

Mont Vernon is a deliberately small town. There is no downtown commercial district, no big-box retail, no stoplights. The town center consists of a few historic buildings, the village school, the new library, and a general store. Residents drive to Milford or Amherst for most errands. This is a feature, not a bug, for the buyers who choose it.

The community culture revolves around:
- **Mont Vernon Village School** — the hub of community life
- **Annual Lamson Farm Day** — a longstanding harvest celebration
- **Mont Vernon Spring Gala** — held May 30, 2026 (11am–2pm)
- **America 250 Commission** — the town has organized community events around the July 4, 2026 national 250th anniversary, with specific emphasis on Mont Vernon's 46 Revolutionary War heroes
- **Conservation Commission & outdoor recreation** — Purgatory Falls trail system, Wah Lum Loop, and hundreds of acres of protected land provide year-round hiking, snowshoeing, and nature access steps from residential neighborhoods

## 3. Recent News (2026)

### The Daland Memorial Library — 40 Years in the Making
*Source: NHPR, Union Leader, February–March 2026*

This is the defining civic story of 2026 in Mont Vernon. The Daland Memorial Library — a $6 million project four decades in the making — opened on February 22, 2026, in a new farmhouse-style building on Lilac Lane.

Key facts:
- An original library committee was formed in **1986** — the building took 40 years to realize
- The final $2 million in funding required **60% voter approval** at Town Meeting; it passed with 61.8%
- **~500 people** attended opening day, representing roughly 1 in 5 Mont Vernon residents — a stunning turnout for any community event
- The building features a full children's area with an enchanted forest theme and fairy cottage reading nook, a dedicated teen space, a book club room, a community meeting room, a kitchen, and a free coffee bar

The library opening is a signal event for the town's trajectory. A community that fights for 40 years to build a library — and then shows up 500 strong to celebrate it — is a community with deep civic investment. This is not a town in decline.

### Town Meeting Actions (February–March 2026)

Mont Vernon held its annual Town Meeting in late February and March 2026. The Budget Committee and Board of Selectmen both convened in the weeks prior. No major zoning controversies or development battles emerged in publicly available coverage — the library funding approval in the preceding year dominated civic energy.

### America 250 Commission

Mont Vernon has established an America 250 Commission to organize community celebrations for July 4, 2026 — the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence. The commission is highlighting Mont Vernon's 46 Revolutionary War heroes and coordinating a community event around the anniversary. This is a meaningful civic moment for any new resident arriving in July 2026.

### Carleton Pond Conservation Project

The Conservation Commission has been working on the Carleton Pond Restoration project, with plans to install informational kiosks and native plantings along the pond corridor. This is part of an ongoing effort to maintain and improve the town's conservation lands.

### Recreation Department

Mont Vernon Recreation hosted its Spring Gala on May 30, 2026. The department runs regular programming for youth and families throughout the year, anchored by the school calendar.
