## 🏘️ Town Overview: Salem, NH at a Glance

Salem is one of the fastest-growing towns in New Hampshire and arguably the most strategically positioned for buyers relocating from Massachusetts. Situated directly on the MA border at the southern end of Rockingham County, Salem sits just 35 miles from Boston and offers immediate access to I-93 — making it the default first stop for commuters and remote workers looking to maximize the NH lifestyle advantage.

With a projected 2026 population of ~31,700, Salem has grown from just 5,000 residents in 1950 into a full-service suburb with serious commercial muscle. The Route 28 corridor generates an estimated $1.8 billion in annual economic activity through tax-free retail, restaurants, and entertainment — and the town's anchor development story is just getting started.

Salem is not Amherst. It's busier, denser, and more commercially oriented — but it's also more accessible price-wise, more dynamic in terms of development, and increasingly attractive to the hybrid-schedule professional class that can now commute two or three days a week rather than five.

- **Days on Market:** ~27–28 days (up from ~22 days last year)
- **Average Offers Received:** 3 per listing
- **Market Competitiveness:** Very Competitive / Seller's Market

**Key Insight for Buyers and Sellers:** Salem is experiencing a mild softening from its 2024–2025 peak — prices are slightly off highs, days on market have lengthened a touch, and the frantic bidding wars of prior years have eased. But the market is still competitive: homes average 3 offers and sell in under a month. For buyers, this is actually the most favorable entry window in several years. For sellers, well-priced homes still move fast. The $318/sq. ft. figure is notable — compared to comparable MA border towns across the river, Salem still represents significant value.

**Salem vs. The Region:**
- Salem median (~$597K) vs. Amherst median (~$739K): ~$142K gap
- Salem median vs. comparable MA towns (Andover, Methuen, Haverhill): Salem often runs $100K–$200K below equivalent square footage across the border
- No MA sales tax on NH purchases adds further appeal for everyday consumer spending

## 📰 Town News & Announcements

### Major Development: Tuscan Village & Rockingham Grand Casino

The former Rockingham Park horse racing track site — once the symbolic heart of Salem's entertainment legacy — is being reborn as one of the most ambitious mixed-use developments in New England.

**Tuscan Village** is a 170-acre project ultimately targeting ~2.8 million square feet of retail, restaurants, hotels, entertainment, office space, and housing. Key 2026 milestones:
- **The Epicurean (Building 1300):** A 6-story, 450,000 sq. ft. mixed-use building targeting May 2026 completion, combining residential units, retail, and community amenities
- **West Village District:** Newly approved by Planning Board (March 2026, 7-0 vote) — will add a medical office, retail/restaurant space, 133 condos, 50 units of workforce housing, and a 150-room hotel
- **The Artisan at Tuscan Village** (Tribute Portfolio Hotel) is already open and drawing regional visitors

**Rockingham Grand Casino:** Churchill Downs Incorporated, in partnership with developers Joe Faro and Sal Lupoli, is investing $180M–$200M to transform 160,000 sq. ft. of the former Mall at Rockingham Park into a full gaming and entertainment destination — featuring 825 historical racing machines, 32 table games, a 900-seat live entertainment venue, and multiple dining concepts.

**Mall at Rockingham Park:** Simon Property Group is actively leasing new tenants. LOFT has already opened; Kendra Scott, OFFLINE by Aerie, Caffe Nero, and Five Below are coming summer/fall 2026. A former Sears Auto Center is being converted into "Game On!" (adult entertainment) and "Loretta's Last Call" (live music venue/restaurant), targeted for summer/fall 2026 completion.

**Route 28 / Rockingham Park Blvd Intersection Improvements** are currently underway — a town-funded infrastructure investment acknowledging the commercial corridor's growth trajectory.

## 💡 Why Buyers Are Choosing Salem

**1. NH Tax Advantages for the Right Buyer**
Salem's pitch is strongest for remote workers, retirees, and NH-based business owners — people who can establish NH residency and avoid MA's 5% income tax entirely. For hybrid commuters (2–3 days/week in Boston), the calculus is increasingly favorable as fewer commuting days reduce MA tax exposure. No NH sales tax adds a real, everyday financial benefit that compounds over time.

**2. The Best Commute Math in Southern NH**
Direct I-93 access. ~35 miles to Boston. Express bus service to Boston's North Station via MBTA commuter connections. Of all NH towns, Salem arguably offers the most direct, reliable Boston commute — which is why it consistently tops relocation lists for MA buyers.

**3. Value Gap vs. Massachusetts**
Comparable homes in Andover, North Andover, Methuen, or Haverhill routinely run $100K–$200K more than equivalent Salem properties. The NH price advantage is real and measurable, and Salem is the most accessible entry point.

**4. A Town in Active Transformation**
The Tuscan Village/Rockingham Grand development story is still early. Buyers purchasing in Salem now are betting on a town whose commercial, hospitality, and entertainment infrastructure is expanding rapidly — which historically correlates with sustained property value appreciation.

**5. Lifestyle Wins**
Canobie Lake Park (steps from suburban neighborhoods), Hedgehog Park, Field of Dreams recreation complex, a robust Community Services rec program, and easy access to both Boston's cultural amenities and NH's lakes and mountains — Salem residents get urban access without urban prices.

## 🏫 Schools & Community Infrastructure

- **Salem School District** serves pre-K through 12 with multiple elementary schools, Woodbury Middle School, and Salem High School
- Salem High School consistently earns solid academic ratings and has active athletic and extracurricular programs
- **Private options** include several parochial and independent schools within easy commute
- **Higher education nearby:** Southern NH University (Manchester, ~25 min), UMass Lowell (~20 min across the border)
- **Healthcare:** Multiple urgent care and specialty clinics along Route 28; major hospitals in Nashua, Manchester, and Greater Boston within 30–45 minutes
- **Public safety:** Active police department managing a growing commercial corridor; town proactively addressing traffic and public safety as Tuscan Village scales up
