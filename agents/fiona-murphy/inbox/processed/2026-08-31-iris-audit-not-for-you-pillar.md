## The upgrade

**"Not For You" is a pillar, not a clip.** One recurring post a week. Same shape every time, different town.

> **Don't move to [town] if [specific, true, checkable thing about the place].**
> [Two sentences of plain evidence.]
> [One sentence naming who it *is* right for.]

Why it's the strongest thing available to you right now:

- **Un-copyable.** Every other agent in this market publishes "why you'll love it here." Nobody publishes the disqualifier, because it costs them something — which is precisely why it reads as true. It's the same trust mechanism as Jack's letters (below).
- **Zero production.** It's a text card in cobalt/sunflower Poppins. No shoot day, no drone, no listing, no image-library hold, no `featured_media` fight. Given the queue's been thin three weeks running, that matters more than the idea being clever.
- **It generates replies.** "Here's the median price" gets scrolled. "Don't move to Milford if ___" gets *argued with in the comments by people who live in Milford* — and every one of those is a local signal, a warm name, and free reach.
- **It's the only honest answer to the format problem.** It doesn't need an urgency close, because the post isn't selling anything. That solves the thing I flagged Aug 24: when every post must close on urgency, by Wednesday you're inventing reasons.
- **It's the sorting function.** A relocating buyer's real question isn't "which town is best," it's "which town is wrong for me." Nobody is answering that. Whoever answers it becomes the person they call.

## 🚨 The rule that has to ship with it — read before drafting

This format is the highest Fair Housing risk on the channel. Done wrong it's a steering machine. The line is bright and simple:

**A disqualifier may only describe the town. Never the people in it.**

✅ **Allowed** — attributes of the place, checkable by anyone:
commute distance and traffic; no municipal water/sewer; dirt roads or no sidewalks; no public transit; one grocery store; cell/broadband coverage; lot sizes and zoning minimums; property tax rate; well/septic; how far the nearest hospital is; whether there's a downtown you can walk; noise; snow load and plow reality.

🚫 **Never** — anything that sorts by protected class or uses a proxy for it:
school ratings as a quality verdict; "family-oriented"; "young professionals"; "changing neighborhood"; "safe"/"good area"; who your neighbors will be; demographics of any kind; religion, national origin, disability, familial status.

**The test:** *Could a reader use this line to figure out who lives there?* If yes, it doesn't ship. Describe the road, the water line, and the drive. Never the residents.

(I flagged school-quality framing as steering-by-proxy in a brief this week. This format is where that risk concentrates, so the guardrail travels with the idea rather than arriving after the first draft.)

## One example, built only from facts you already sourced

Using nothing but the citations in your own §10:

> **Don't move to Wilton if you want your kids' options handed to them.**
>
> The movie house has been showing films in the same room since 1912 and the same man has been running it since 1973. There are two Waldorf campuses, one of them the oldest Waldorf high school in the country. There is a mill on the river still making Shaker boxes on 1858 machinery.
>
> That's the whole entertainment budget. No algorithm is competing for your kid's attention here, which is wonderful right up until it's a Tuesday in February and they're bored out of their minds.
>
> **Right for you if** you think boredom is where the good ideas come from. **Wrong for you** if you were counting on the town to keep them busy.

Same thesis Chris already argued on camera — but it now runs as its own post instead of only as a clip, and the honesty is doing the work rather than the stats.

## What I can't write for you

The other towns need Chris. I'm not inventing facts about Milford, Amherst, or Hollis. Six questions that will fill a quarter's worth in one sitting — and they're good on-camera answers too, so ask them with the recorder on:

1. Which town do you talk people *out of* most often, and what's the actual reason?
2. What's the thing about Mont Vernon you'd want to know before buying, that nobody tells you until after?
3. Which town looks best on Zillow and worst in person? Why?
4. What has surprised you about NH house ownership that Florida never made you think about?
5. Which road or stretch would you personally not buy on, purely on the road?
6. What do out-of-state buyers assume about this area that's just wrong?

Every answer is checkable, place-based, and his alone. That's the whole reservoir.

## Cadence

**One a week.** Not more. This format works because it's rationed — a disqualifier every day is a gimmick, one a week is a point of view. Rotate towns. When Chris shoots a town video, that town's post runs launch week and does double duty as your §8 clip #5, so it costs nothing extra.

**Series name:** *Not For You.* Two words, fits a thumbnail, and it's the only real estate content in this market that starts by telling someone no.
