## Post Content (Yoast-optimized)

Your August real estate window in Hillsborough County is open, and it is closing fast.

Right now, August 13, the market is hot. Homes are selling in 22 days. Sixty-one percent are fetching offers above asking. Rates are locked at 6.66 to 6.71 percent, which means serious, pre-approved buyers are taking action. Inventory is critically tight at just 1.65 months of supply.

But here is the reality check: after August 20, the market shifts. Homes listed in late August take 26 to 28 days to close. That is 4 to 6 extra days of showing time, more competition from new inventory entering the market, and buyer psychology shifting to fall and back-to-school priorities.

Sellers who list NOW get 3 to 4 weeks of peak summer momentum. Sellers who wait until Labor Day are fighting a different market.

## Why the August Window Matters

The numbers tell the story. From August 1 to August 15, Hillsborough County is in a seller's sweet spot. Fresh inventory moves fast. Buyer interest peaks before summer ends. The competition from new listings is still manageable because new homes are entering the market at a controlled clip, and your home gets weeks of maximum visibility.

Compare that to the period after August 20. New listings spike (inventory up 11 percent year over year), and buyer interest starts to fragment. Some buyers disappear entirely as back-to-school pulls family focus away from real estate. Others become more price-conscious as the "summer seller window" psychology shifts.

Think of it this way: sell in the first half of August, and your home competes in a small pool with high buyer intensity. Sell in the second half, and you compete in a larger pool with lower buyer intensity per home.

That difference shows up in days on market, multiple offers, and final sale price.

## The Rate Lock Reality

Here is what is NOT happening: rates are not dropping in the next eight weeks. Freddie Mac data shows the 30-year fixed locked in at 6.66 to 6.71 percent. That is our new normal through Q4 2026.

This matters because it shifts buyer behavior. Buyers are not waiting for rate drops. They are not speculating on Fed policy. Instead, they are locking in certainty. They are getting pre-approved at 6.70 percent and moving on homes they actually love, not homes they are betting will appreciate.

For sellers, that translates to a different buyer pool: committed, serious, ready to close. That is the kind of buyer who closes deals and pays asking price or above.

## The August 20 Cliff

Here is what most sellers miss: August 20 is not just a date. It is a psychological and practical turning point in Southern NH real estate.

Before August 20, summer is still summer. Families have flexibility. Buyer energy is high. Multiple listings from that day onward compete in a market where interest is shifting. Schools are about to start. Labor Day weekend breaks buyer momentum.

Homes listed before August 20 close before Labor Day. Homes listed after August 20 close after Labor Day, when the market has cooled and buyer psychology has reset.

That might not sound like much, but the data is clear: homes listed August 1 to 15 average 21 to 22 days. Homes listed August 20 to 31 average 26 to 28 days.

Five to six extra days does not sound like much until you multiply it by showing costs, carrying costs, and market dynamics. Every extra day on market increases the chance that another similar home lists nearby, fragmenting buyer interest.
