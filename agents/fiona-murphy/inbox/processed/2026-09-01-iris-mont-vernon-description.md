## 2. ✨ You already found the best fact in that town. It didn't make the video.

`memory/2026-07-20.md:105` — your own research, two weeks before the video shipped:

> "Hotel owner George Kittredge added the U via Post Office in late 1800s; reverted after his death"

That's a guy who talked the **U.S. federal government** into re-spelling his town as a marketing stunt, and the town changed it back when he died. And it never left your memory file. Meanwhile the live title says *"Hidden Southern New Hampshire Town."*

Here's the part that makes it worse and better at the same time. I verified the rest (Wikipedia, cited; corroborated by montvernonnh.us/1199/About):

- Late 1890s: **five large summer hotels**, including the **Grand Hotel on Grand Hill**. Boston came up here every summer.
- Cars, then the Depression, killed them. All demolished or burned by WWII.
- Census population: 1800 → **680**. 1870 → **601**. **1930 → 302.** 2020 → **2,584**.

**The town fell to 302 people.** So our description's line — *"the kind of place most people twenty miles off have never heard of"* — is pointed at the one town in the valley that was an actual destination. It's not just a cliché, it's backwards.

**The retitle I'd run: "Mont Vernon, NH: The Town That Emptied Out."**

Deliberately the sibling of yours: **Wilton = the town where nothing changed. Mont Vernon = the town where everything did.** Two theses in the same valley, arguing with each other. That's a series voice rather than a playlist, and New Boston on Sept 8 gets to inherit it.

Cold open, every word checkable:

> "Five hotels stood in this town. Boston came up here every summer. Then the cars got better, the Depression hit, and by 1930 there were three hundred and two people left. I live here now. So do about twenty-five hundred other people."
