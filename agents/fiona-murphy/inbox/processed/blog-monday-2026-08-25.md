<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The numbers tell the story. Across New Hampshire, median home prices have climbed to record levels in 2026. Hillsborough County is holding steady around $540,917 for a median sale. Statewide, the median sits at $533,106, up a solid 2.9% year-over-year. Meanwhile, the national median home price is $410,700, appreciation of just 0.5%.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>This is the rare market still moving upward. Inventory is growing too. According to data from <a href="https://www.nhar.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the New Hampshire Association of Realtors</a>, single-family home listings across the state jumped 9.7% year-over-year in July 2026. More homes. Higher prices. Stronger fundamentals.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} --><h2>The Price Band That Matters: $500k to $900k</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Not all price bands are equal right now. Understanding where the market is shifting will help you make smarter decisions, whether you are buying or selling.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Homes under $500,000 in towns like Nashua, Manchester, and Hooksett are moving fast. Multiple offers are still common. First-time buyers and downsizers are competing aggressively in this range. If you are listing here, demand is strong. If you are buying, move quickly and be prepared.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The $500,000 to $900,000 range is where the real shift is happening. In Bedford, Amherst, Hollis, and Bow, the market has just tipped toward buyers. Days on market are climbing. Sellers are more flexible on inspections, appraisals, and closing costs. Buyers in this sweet spot now have real negotiating power. If you have been waiting for your moment, this is it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Above $900,000, activity is lighter. Premium neighborhoods are still selling, but the pace has slowed and days on market are climbing. This is a market for patient sellers and thoughtful buyers.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

Remote work has made even that flexible.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Schools matter, and New Hampshire schools consistently rank among the best in the nation. These towns deliver on the promise of good schools, strong communities, and that New England charm.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>There is also something intangible about New England in late summer. Four genuine seasons. Charming downtown districts with coffee shops and farmers markets. Access to hiking, lakes, and outdoor life. That quality-of-life factor that money alone cannot buy.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Buyers from flatter, warmer states and from crowded urban areas look at Southern New Hampshire and see something worth the move. Home prices reflect that preference.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>August and September are peak relocation months in New England. People move before the school year starts or in late summer to settle in before September. By October, many relocators have already closed. By November and December, the market slows for the winter.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>That means right now—through September—is your window. Inventory is climbing. Buyer room to negotiate is opening. Interest rates are holding steady. Homes in Southern New Hampshire are appreciating.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>If you close before the end of the year, you also benefit from year-end tax planning. Mortgage interest deductions, property tax deductions, and capital gains strategies all favor a late-2026 closing.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The market is moving. The prices are appreciating. The next 90 days are the peak window. Wait too long, and you will miss it.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Here is why this matters: every quarter point in rate matters over 30 years.
