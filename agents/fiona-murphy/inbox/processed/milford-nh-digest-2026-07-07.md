## Market Context

The Milford market has shifted meaningfully since May. What was a 12-13 day sprint is now a 30-40 day process — still healthy, but buyers have regained breathing room. The $99K list-to-sale gap from Spring has tightened as sellers recalibrate expectations to meet a more measured buyer pool.

Hillsborough County's Q1 median of $540,917 shows a modest ↓1.7% YoY correction — not a crash, a cooldown. Statewide inventory is up 13.3% YoY, giving buyers more options and sellers more competition. At 6.43–6.51% mortgage rates, a $485K purchase at 20% down runs roughly $2,475/month — meaningful friction compared to the low-rate era, but demand from the Boston corridor remains steady.

**Prospecting angles:**

- **For sellers:** The window of maximum leverage has narrowed — but well-priced, well-presented homes in Milford still move. Now is the time to price right from day one, not to test the market.
- **For buyers/relocators:** This is the first real opportunity in 18+ months to negotiate. Boston-area professionals priced out of the suburbs are still moving to NH; they now have time to make thoughtful offers.
- **For investors:** Slower absorption + rising inventory = opportunity to acquire at more reasonable multiples. Milford's rental demand (Route 101/I-93 commuter base) remains intact.

## Community Profile

**Milford, NH — "The Granite Town"**
Population ~15,000 | On the Souhegan River | ~45 min. to Boston via Rte. 101 to I-93

Milford sits at a quiet inflection point in mid-summer: Fourth of July energy carrying into a full calendar of outdoor events, the farmers market hitting peak season, and the Milford Oval — the heart of the town common — buzzing with community life. It's the kind of town where a Tuesday evening concert draws three generations of families with lawn chairs and dogs, and neighbors actually know each other's names.

**July Highlights:**

| Event / Feature | Details |
|----------------|---------|
| **Milford Farmers Market** | Saturdays throughout July, Town Oval area; local produce, artisan vendors, live music |
| **Milford Oval Summer Concert Series** | Free outdoor concerts on the Town Oval; family-friendly; typically runs late June through August |
| **Fourth of July Community Celebrations** | Early July fireworks, parade traditions, and post-holiday community gatherings along the Souhegan River corridor |
| **Milford Recreation Department Summer Programs** | Youth camps, adult fitness classes, and family programming — a consistent quality-of-life anchor for families evaluating the town |
| **Ogie Brewing Co.** | Year-round local anchor; summer patio season draws the 30s-40s demographic who want small-town feel with craft food/drink culture |

**What makes Milford distinctive:**
- Historic granite industry heritage preserved in downtown architecture
- Souhegan River recreation (fishing, kayaking, trails)
- Strong school system relative to price point — a key driver for young families
- Economic Revitalization Zones offering state tax credits for business development
- Compact, walkable downtown with genuine local character (not strip-mall sprawl)
