### Full Post (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Google Business)

The 30 year fixed averaged 6.66 percent this week, essentially where it sat last week at 6.65
percent (Freddie Mac PMMS, week ending August 27). One basis point. Freddie's own headline was
"Mortgage Rates Hold Steady."

We are not going to turn that into urgency, because it is not urgency. A one basis point move
is noise. Anyone telling you to rush a decision this week because of what rates did is selling
you something.

Here is what actually changed, and it is not the rate.

Active inventory in New Hampshire hit 2,992 homes in July, up 16 percent year over year and the
highest level in roughly seven years (NHAR). In Hillsborough County, active listings reached
1,494, up 8 percent (Redfin). That is the real shift this cycle, and it is the one that works in
a buyer's favor. More homes means you can compare, inspect properly, and walk away from one that
does not fit.

On where rates go next, we will give you our source rather than our opinion: Fannie Mae's
forecast has the 30 year finishing 2026 below 6 percent. Forecasts are forecasts. But we are not
going to tell you rates will not fall when our own cited forecast says they will.

One more thing we will not smooth over. Statewide, New Hampshire set an all time record median
single family price of $580,000 in July, up 5.5 percent (NHAR). But Nashua's median came in at
$576,500, down 2.7 percent year over year, and Mont Vernon is down year over year as well. The
regional average is not your town.

If you want to know what your budget actually reaches, in your town, with the real numbers behind
it, get in touch. We will pull it properly.
