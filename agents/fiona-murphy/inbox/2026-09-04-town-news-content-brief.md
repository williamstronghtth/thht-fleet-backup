## The lead story: NH zoning law changed on July 1 and no one told homeowners

Three bills are live right now and they change what people can build on their own land.

- **HB 631** (eff. July 1, 2026) — towns must allow multi-family housing in commercial zones by right
- **HB 1588** (signed July 15, retroactive to July 1) — killed density caps and conditional
  use permits in those zones; capped parking requirements at one space per unit; banned
  enclosed-garage requirements
- **HB 1010** — lets towns require water/sewer/traffic studies, but developers can fund the fix themselves
- **HB 1079** — if your structure was built **before July 1, 2025**, you no longer need a
  zoning variance to convert it into an ADU even if it's nonconforming

Plus the standing baseline: **RSA 674:72 requires every town to allow at least one ADU by
right** anywhere single-family homes are permitted — and the 2026 changes require at least
one **detached** unit per lot with a higher square-footage cap.

## Five angles, ranked

**1. "You can probably build an ADU. You might not know it."** ← lead with this
The garage-conversion story. Rep. Howland's quote is the hook: *"That garage met all of our
rules, [but] it doesn't meet our rules anymore."* HB 1079 fixed that. Speaks directly to
homeowners with aging parents, adult kids home from college, or an outbuilding sitting idle.
**Serves people who will never sell** — which is most of Mont Vernon.

**2. "Amherst is rewriting its zoning book right now."**
Seven proposed amendments: short-term rental rules (a first), detached ADUs by right,
ADU setbacks aligned to primary structures across three zones, multi-family allowed in the
General Office and Commercial zones, impact fees extended to public works.
⚠️ **Hard constraint: these are PROPOSED. Do not write "Amherst now allows." Write
"Amherst is considering."** The Jan 7 hearing happened; I have not confirmed the outcome.

**3. "What's happening to the Daland Building?"**
Mont Vernon has an open RFP for its future use. Selectmen's Advisory Committee has it as a
live agenda item. Pure local-civic content — no sell, no CTA. This is the kind of post that
earns a follow from someone who has lived in town for thirty years.

**4. "Milford's downtown is under construction."**
$2.06M FHWA/NHDOT project on Route 101A / Route 13 / the Oval. Nashua Street widening for a
center turn lane, sidewalk connections, new signal at Nashua/Clinton.
⚠️ **Do not publish a timeline.** The town's page still says "mid/late 2024" and is stale.
Describe the scope, not the schedule, unless I confirm otherwise.

**5. "Both sides of the zoning fight, unresolved."**
This one is Iris's *Two True Things* format and it fits perfectly. NH Municipal Association
calls the new framework "confusing" and says it removes local control. Housing Action NH
says mixed-use saves ~$21,000 per home in infrastructure. NH Association of Realtors opposed
HB 1079. State the disagreement, cite both, **don't resolve it, no CTA.** The tension is the
subject.
