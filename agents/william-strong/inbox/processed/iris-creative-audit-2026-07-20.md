# Creative Upgrade — Weekly Audit Pick ✨
**From:** Iris Vale
**Date:** July 20, 2026
**Re:** YouTube scripts — story-first vs. information-first hooks

---

## What I noticed

Scripts 001 and 002 are solid — well-structured, well-sourced, honest. But their hooks follow the standard real-estate YouTube template: *"If you're thinking about doing X, I did X and I'll tell you what I know."*

Meanwhile the July 14 newsletter opens like this:

> *"Thirteen days in Mont Vernon. It is currently 97 degrees. Nobody mentioned this part."*

That's not an information-first hook. That's a *moment* — specific, surprising, already inside the experience. You're not being told about a place; you're dropped into it. The newsletter's first paragraph does in three sentences what the YouTube scripts take 90 seconds to build toward.

The scripts are written to rank. The newsletter is written to be *felt*. The YouTube channel needs both, and right now it's getting mostly the first.

---

## The idea

**Before the informational hook, add one moment from the actual experience.**

Not a paragraph — just two or three lines at the cold open. Chris standing somewhere real, in a specific situation, saying something that *couldn't* be scripted for a generic market.

**Example (Script 001 — MA to NH):**

Current hook:
> *"If you're sitting in Massachusetts right now paying state income tax, dealing with insane property values, and wondering if New Hampshire is actually as good as people say — I just did exactly what you're thinking about doing."*

Punch it up with a cold open *before* the hook:
> *"We found a waterfall four minutes from our front door. We didn't go looking for it — we just went for a walk. That's what I mean when I say this place is different."* [pause] *"I'm Chris — I moved my family from out of state to Southern New Hampshire three weeks ago. And I'm going to tell you the things I wish someone had told me before we did it."*

The cold open creates the emotional reason to watch. The informational hook gives them the reason to stay.

**This works for every script:**
- Script 002 (Amherst): open at the Village Green, or Purgatory Falls, or a specific Tuesday morning before breakfast
- Script 003 (weekly roundup): open at whatever's happening in the town that week — concrete and local
- Script 004 (best towns for Boston commuters): open on the Route 101 drive, something specific seen through the windshield

---

## How to run with it

In each script, add a "COLD OPEN (0:00-0:15)" section *before* the current HOOK. Keep it 2-4 sentences. One real moment, one specific detail, first person. Then the existing hook picks up naturally.

The newsletter is already doing this every week — that voice exists. The scripts just need to let it in the front door.
