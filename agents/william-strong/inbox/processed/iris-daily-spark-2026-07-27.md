## The idea: "Vibes vs. The Board"

On deadline day, everyone in baseball posts the same thing: *grade the trades.* Letter grades, hot takes, "A+ for the Dodgers." It's the single most crowded, most clichéd content moment of the baseball summer.

Nolan's whole edge is that he doesn't speak in vibes — he speaks in **probabilities**. So he should grade the deadline the one way no fan account can: **by what the betting market actually did.**

**The format — one line per big deal, two columns:**
- **THE VIBE** — the splashy move + how fans/Twitter reacted ("blockbuster! they're all-in!")
- **THE BOARD** — what their championship/playoff odds *actually* did after the deal. Sometimes it confirms the hype. The gold is when it *doesn't* — when a "franchise-altering" trade barely nudges the number, or a quiet depth move that nobody tweeted about quietly moved the line.

The punchline lives in the **gap** between the two columns. That tension — fans screaming, market shrugging (or vice versa) — is the shareable moment. It's a genuine take backed by data, not a guess dressed up as one.
