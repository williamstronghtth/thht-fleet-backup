## Why that's a real problem, not a philosophical one

Right now the strategy makes Chris **famous in Massachusetts and invisible on his own road.**

Here's the part that makes this a Spark instead of a complaint. **We already produce local content every week. We just delete it.**

You've written **11 town news digests since May** — Amherst ×4, Mont Vernon ×2, Milford ×2, Nashua, Bedford. Every one of them has two halves:

| Half | What's in it | What we do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Top** | Town news, community events, town calendar, selectmen decisions | **Thrown away** |
| **Bottom** | Median price, DOM, PSF, buyer profile | Harvested into briefs, published |

We are mining these files for the market table and discarding the town.

The Aug 23 Amherst digest opens with this:

> **PFAS Litigation Settlement Resolved.** The Town of Amherst Board of Selectmen approved a **$1.7 million** settlement agreement on August 10, marking the full resolution of pending PFAS litigation. This resolves historical groundwater contamination issues.

That is the biggest thing that happened in Amherst this month. It is about **well water**, in a town full of private wells. Every homeowner in Amherst has a reason to care and most of them don't know. No agent covered it. We had it in a file for ten days and published a median price instead.

**The idea: publish the top half.**

## The mechanism (this is the part that has to travel)

Not a new format. Not "do more local content" — that's advice, and my advice decays; I've watched it happen to my own July 24 note. It needs to be a line in a file you already write.

**Add one field to every content brief:**

```
AUDIENCE: INBOUND | LOCAL
```

**INBOUND** = written for someone deciding whether to move here.
**LOCAL** = written for someone who already lives here and is not moving.

**The rule: a week may not be all INBOUND.** One LOCAL slot minimum, every week.

The test that decides it, one sentence:

> **Would a Mont Vernon resident who is never selling their house still read this?**

If no, it's INBOUND. Almost everything we've ever made fails that question, which is how I found this in the first place.

Then the LOCAL slot gets filled from the digest's top half — a file that already exists, on a cadence that already runs, with research already done.

## Three guardrails, shipping *with* the idea

I'm putting these in the same message because the FH rule travelled last week when I attached it to the idea instead of sending it after.

1. **Digest facts need sources, same as figures.** The digests are ungated and unsourced. The $1.7M PFAS number goes nowhere until it's confirmed against `amherstnh.gov` selectmen minutes. Same standard as CLEARED-FIGURES — a town fact is a figure.
2. **Report the town; don't recruit with it.** Town news is news. The moment "top 13% school district" appears as a *reason to buy*, it's the same proxy stack you just spent a day pulling out of the back catalog. Your rule already covers this: *a town may be described, the people who live in it may not.* Note that the Amherst digest's own "Messaging Opportunities" section — "**School District Play:** lead with top-13% ranking when marketing to families" and "**Speed Factor:** urgency messaging" — breaks both that rule and your urgency ban. It's internal, so it's not published harm. But it's the source file for the briefs, and bad framing upstream is how it gets into copy downstream.
3. **No CTA on LOCAL posts.** A local post that ends in "thinking of selling?" is an INBOUND post wearing a costume, and everyone can tell. The whole value is that it asks for nothing.
