## The idea

### **"5 Things I Got Wrong About Moving to New Hampshire"**

Chris, on camera, listing the assumptions he brought from Florida that the Souhegan Valley corrected. Not a brochure. A confession.

**Why the timing is the whole mechanism.** At two months he still remembers being confused. At a year he won't — he'll have absorbed all of it and will sound like every other agent who's "been here forever," which is precisely the voice a relocating buyer distrusts. His newness is a wasting asset. This video spends it before it evaporates. Same structural logic as the Same Frame pitch: the shot expires.

**Why it inverts the channel instead of extending it.** Three videos in, every one has said *here's what's great about this place*. This is the first one that costs him something to say — and cost is what makes a claim believable. It's the same principle that made Fiona's published correction land, and the same reason "nine houses isn't a law, it's nine houses" was the strongest line in yesterday's pitch. Admitting the limit is what buys the credibility.

**Why it's search-native.** "Moving to New Hampshire, what nobody tells you" is the exact Pozek relocation query set. "Things I got wrong" promises information asymmetry rather than a tour. It ranks and it converts.

**Why it unblocks us.** No listing (the "Who's This House For?" blocker, now day 6). No drone, no airspace. No archival permissions. No pull from an image library that still contains zero verifiable Southern NH locations. He shoots this in his own driveway with the 70D. And five wrong assumptions = **five self-contained Shorts, five captions, five newsletter P.S. lines** — which is the direct answer to "nothing scheduled in Late for next week," now the third week running and top of Fiona's own list.

## What I am NOT doing: inventing the content

I don't know what Chris got wrong, and I'm not going to make it up. Yesterday I refused to let two zero-DOM sales ship unverified; Thursday I refused to name Milford landmarks I hadn't confirmed. Same rule here.

**So the deliverable is the structure and the questions that pull the truth out of him.** Chris answers these on a voice memo in the car; whoever edits builds the video from his actual answers.

**Interview prompts — phrased to get a story, not a fact:**

1. What did you assume about a drive time here that turned out to be wrong? (101 / 101A / the Nashua approach)
2. What has the *house itself* done that Florida never prepared you for? (well, septic, propane vs. oil, the generator question, the driveway grade)
3. Which town did you think you'd live your life in, versus the one you actually drive to for groceries, gas, and the hardware store? — *the "where you sleep vs. where your life happens" gap is the single most useful thing a relocating buyer can learn and no town profile ever covers it*
4. What did you believe about "no income tax" before your first property tax bill?
5. What did you brace for that turned out easy? — *one positive, or it reads as five complaints*
6. What did you get wrong about how you'd meet people here? (transfer station, dump sticker, the town Facebook group)

## The lead Short — and the cross-pollination underneath it

Cut **#4 first**. Here's why it's the strongest thing in the piece.

Every buyer moving up from Massachusetts shops the mortgage rate obsessively. Right now that's 6.66% versus 6.65% last week — a one-basis-point move, which Fiona herself correctly called noise on Aug 27. Meanwhile the NH property tax rate is set **town by town**, varies meaningfully between towns that are eight minutes apart, and almost nobody shops it at all.

In Chris's own language: **everyone hammers the heavily-shopped line and ignores the one nobody's priced.** The posted mortgage rate is the efficient market. The town tax rate is the soft number. That's not a metaphor I'm decorating with — it's arithmetic that can move a monthly payment more than a quarter point of rate does, and it's the reason this video earns a real estate conversation without a single line of sales copy in it.

**Verification required before this ships:** pull current equalized rates for the six market towns from NH DRA. Use no number I haven't verified and Chris hasn't checked — I'm not supplying figures here on purpose. He should speak from his own bill and tell viewers to check their specific town, which is both true and the actual useful advice.
