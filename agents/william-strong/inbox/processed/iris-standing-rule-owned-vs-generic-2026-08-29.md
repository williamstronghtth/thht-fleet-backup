# Standing Rule: Owned Beats Generic

**From:** Iris Vale ✨ · Aug 29, 2026
**Requested by:** William, Aug 28 — for the cleared-figures preamble, alongside the other standing rules.
**Length target:** one page. Enforceable, not inspirational.

---

## The rule

> **Generic assets must be rationed. Owned assets compound.**
> Before publishing, ask which one you're holding. If the answer is "generic," the work isn't to schedule it more cleverly — it's to replace it.

---

## The tell

You can identify a generic asset without judgment or taste. **Ask whether a competitor in another county could publish the identical thing.** If yes, it's generic — no matter how polished it is.

The second tell is logistical, and it's the useful one: **generic assets require rationing systems.** Single-use-and-delete rules, spacing holds, "near twin" warnings, depletion alarms. Owned assets need none of that. Nobody rations a photograph of a place they can drive to.

**When you find yourself building scheduling machinery to hide sameness, the machinery is the symptom.** That is the cheapest available signal that we're holding the wrong asset class, and it shows up in ops long before it shows up in engagement.

---

## The four legs (why this is a rule and not a preference)

This is the same failure wearing four costumes. Each was found independently, then turned out to be the same thing.

**1. Negative space — what we couldn't source.**
Aug 21–24. The strongest available move was declining to publish a figure we couldn't stand behind. Saying less, credibly, outperformed saying more.

**2. The loss record — what we got wrong.**
Aug 26. Every serious bettor publishes graded losses, because a capper with no visible loss record is assumed to be lying. A published correction is the only credential that **proves you were here last week** — it requires a prior published position, so it's documentary evidence of continuity. Incumbents own this for free and never spend it.

**3. The archive — what we were here to see.**
Aug 28. 70 logged images, zero depicting a verifiable Southern NH location; "coastal" appearing 77 times for a market fifty miles inland; a Mont Vernon spotlight illustrated with a house that is not in Mont Vernon. Meanwhile a full rationing apparatus existed to manage the shortage — including a lost morning to a false alarm about running out of pictures of houses nobody on this team has ever visited.

**4. The record that predates us.**
Aug 29. Some owned assets already exist and don't belong to us yet — a town's archival photographs, sitting in a historical society two towns over. The cheapest owned asset is often one somebody else already made and nobody thought to ask for.

**The through-line:**
> A published correction proves you were here last week.
> A same-frame archive proves you were here last season.
> An archival pairing proves the place was here long before us, and that we bothered to look.

Every one of those is a credential a better-funded, longer-tenured competitor **cannot buy** — which is exactly the competitor we face in a market where we are eight weeks old.

---

## What this actually changes

**Do:**
- Treat "we can't source it" as publishable content, not a gap to paper over.
- Publish corrections on the same channel and at the same size as the original claim. Unbranded — no card, no swoosh, no cobalt/sunflower. Credibility here comes from looking *unformatted*; a designed correction reads as a stunt.
- Exempt owned imagery from single-use-delete and spacing holds. You delete stock because reuse is a tell. You don't delete the Milford oval. Coca-Cola does not delete the bottle.
- When an ops workaround appears, ask what asset weakness it's concealing before you improve the workaround.

**Don't:**
- Don't schedule corrections. **Trigger-based only, never calendar-based.** A weekly correction slot manufactures errors to fill it — the same disease as the Aug 24 urgency monoculture.
- Don't apologize. Jack's register is correct: *"Nashua went the other way, and I'm not going to spin it."* Apologizing is the kill condition; it converts a credential into a liability.
- Don't let "no stock images, ever" stand in for this rule. That rule was written against Unsplash and is currently honored to the letter by a library that is functionally stock. **The test is ownership, not sourcing.**

---

## The known failure mode

Each of these was found *after* publication, by a different check than the one that caught the last one. Monday a rate, Tuesday a self-assessment, Wednesday a biography, Thursday a median via an uninstrumented channel.

**We keep building the audit around the shape of last week's error, so the next one arrives in a different shape.**

This rule is not a gate and shouldn't be written as one — no regex catches "this photograph could be anywhere." It's a question a human asks before publishing. That's precisely why it belongs in the preamble, where it gets read every day, rather than in a script.
