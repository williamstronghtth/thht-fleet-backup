## The Idea: "First 30 Days" — A YouTube Series That Can Only Happen Once

Seven days from now, Chris arrives in Souhegan Valley as an actual new resident. That window — July 1 through July 31 — is the most valuable, uncopyable content asset he'll ever have in this market.

**The series:** Chris shoots 60–90 seconds on his phone, once or twice a week, documenting the actual experience of arriving. Not the market data version — the human version. The coffee shop he ends up at. The backroad he found. The conversation at the hardware store where someone asked what he does and he said "I just moved here and I'm also a real estate agent." The Amherst 4th of July crowd.

No script. No production lift. Raw and first-person.

**Why this wins:**

In sports betting, there's a concept: *edge closes once the market figures it out.* Chris's "new to Souhegan Valley" edge expires on August 1. After that, he's just another agent who lives here. Right now, he's the one who just showed up — and small New England towns pay attention to who just showed up. Documenting it in real-time turns a one-time moment into a permanent trust asset on YouTube.

Nobody can copy this. An agent who's been in Amherst for ten years can't make this video. A competitor moving into NH from outside can't make this video. It's a narrow window only Chris has.

**How to execute:**

- Fiona packages each clip with consistent text overlay: *"New to Souhegan Valley, NH — Week [X]"*
- Same thumbnail format every episode (Chris's face, consistent location text, THHT cobalt)
- No prep required — just Chris with his phone and his actual life
- Clips feed the YouTube channel AND get cut into Reels (repurpose automatically)
- A two-month series: 6–8 episodes, done by Labor Day

**The anchor frame Chris can use on camera:** *"I've helped a lot of people move to new places. I never really understood what that first month felt like — until now."*
