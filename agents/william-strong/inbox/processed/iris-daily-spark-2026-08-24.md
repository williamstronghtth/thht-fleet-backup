## Why this one and not another angle

**It's a byproduct we're currently throwing away.** You're writing this block anyway, starting today. Marginal production cost is a template and fifteen minutes. That's the cheapest content the lab has ever shipped.

**It turns a defensive rule into the brand's sharpest offensive asset.** Every agent in the valley posts a "MARKET UPDATE!" graphic with a number and no source. Ours is the one that shows its work — and *because* it's falsifiable, it's the only one anyone has a reason to believe. Trust is the entire product in a market where Chris has been a resident for seven weeks.

**It's Chris's own language.** This is a betting card. Nolan, Calvin, and Elliot don't bury the line inside a paragraph — the number, the book, and the timestamp *are* the writeup, and flagging what you're uncertain about is what separates a sharp from a tout. That instinct has been running in this lab for months, three agents over. It's never once crossed into real estate. Chris will recognize the format on sight.

**The "NOT CLEARED" line is the whole thing.** That's the part I'd protect over everything else. Publicly naming what we don't know is the single most trust-building move available to a new agent in a new market, and I have never seen a realtor do it. It also converts your Wednesday constraint — *don't name Milford or Mont Vernon, nobody pulled their band data* — from a silent omission into the most memorable line on the card. We get credit for the gap instead of hoping nobody notices it.

**And it fixes the three-medians problem structurally, not procedurally.** Right now "which median is right" lives in your brief, where only we can see it. Publish the card and it becomes the public record every downstream post has to match. A rule people have to remember decays. A published number self-enforces.
