# Fiona's Feedback: Neighbor Intro Letter

**To:** William Strong

---

## Overall Read

The bones are good. The intent is right. But right now it reads like a LinkedIn post got folded up and mailed. It's professional when it should be personal. Here's where it falls flat and how I'd fix it.

---

## What's Flat or Too Safe

**1. "We chose this town intentionally, and we're here to stay."**
This is the most interesting sentence in the letter and it's completely empty. WHY Mont Vernon? What made them drive up, see the town, and say "this is it"? That answer is the personality. Without it, this line just sounds like someone reassuring a landlord they won't skip out on rent.

**2. "I'm not writing to pitch you anything."**
Classic defensive overcorrection. The moment you say "I'm not pitching you," you've pitched the idea of a pitch. Cut it entirely. Let the tone carry that message.

**3. "Welcome to knowing me."**
Trying to be clever, landing slightly awkward. It's a little too self-satisfied for a first impression. A neighbor receiving this might raise an eyebrow. Save the wit for after the relationship exists.

**4. The whole letter is about Chris.**
There's no warmth extended toward the neighbor. One line acknowledging them, their community, what Chris admires about the town or neighborhood would shift this from "here's my bio" to "I see you and I'm glad we're neighbors."

---

## Where to Inject Personality

**The "why NH" story is the missing heart of this letter.** Whatever pulled them from Volusia County to a small New Hampshire town is genuinely interesting. People will want to know. That story, told in one or two real sentences, makes this letter memorable instead of polished-but-forgettable.

**A human detail about the family.** Kids? Dogs? Something they're looking forward to in NH (seasons, hiking, the quiet)? One specific detail makes Chris a person, not a brand.

**The coffee line is the warmest moment, lean in.** "If you ever want to grab coffee" is the realest thing in the draft. Give it more room. Maybe something like: "My door is genuinely open, and I mean that in the old-fashioned way."

---

## Suggested Line Swaps

| Current | Suggested Direction |
|---|---|
| "We chose this town intentionally, and we're here to stay." | Replace with the actual reason — once Chris shares it. This is the line that can make the whole letter. |
| "I'm not writing to pitch you anything." | Cut entirely. |
| "Welcome to knowing me. I hope I can return the favor." | Flip it: "I'm looking forward to knowing you. That's the only reason I'm writing." |

**Opening:** Could be warmer. Instead of leading with his name and address (very formal), consider leading with the move itself: "We just landed in Mont Vernon, 30 Dow Road, and I wanted to introduce myself the old-fashioned way."

---

## Sign-Off

Drop "The Hoover Home Team" from the signature. In a personal neighbor letter, a business name undercuts everything the letter is trying to do. It's the last thing they read and it tips the whole thing into marketing territory.

Just "Chris" or "Chris Hoover" is right. If he wants one line underneath, a simple "Real estate, if you ever need it. Neighbor, always." hits the balance he's going for without feeling like a business card.

---

## Summary

The letter needs:
1. The real "why NH / why Mont Vernon" story (waiting on Chris for this)
2. One human family detail
3. Cut the defensive "not pitching" line
4. More warmth toward the neighbor, less résumé
5. Drop the team name from the sign-off

Once Chris fills in the personal details, this can be genuinely great. The instinct is right. It just needs to breathe.
