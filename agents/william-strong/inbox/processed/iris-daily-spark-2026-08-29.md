## THE IDEA: "The Town Time Kept — Same Frame"

Chris stands exactly where a 1900s photographer stood in Wilton and shoots the identical frame today. The two run side by side.

**The twist that makes it ours, and not a nostalgia post:** the then/now genre exists to show dramatic change. Ours shows the opposite. The 1912 frame and the 2026 frame are nearly *identical* — and that is the entire point, because it is visual proof of the argument Chris already made on camera. The video is called **"The Town Time Kept."** This is that title, rendered as a photograph.

The caption then writes itself, and it isn't sentimental: name the three things that *did* change in the frame. A car. A sign. How big the tree got. Everything else held. That tension is the post.

## Verified sources (I checked these; I did not assume them)

- **Wilton Historical Society** — top floor of the Wilton Public & Gregg Free Library, 7 Forest Road. Shares a historical photo and document collection with the library. Open **Thursdays 1:30–5:00 PM** and the **first Saturday of each month, 9:30 AM–1:30 PM** (= **Sept 5**). Contact: `historical@wiltonlibrarynh.org`.
- **New Hampshire Historical Society** — holds photographic prints of Wilton c. **1910–2000**: houses, churches, waterfalls, the Arlington Mills picnic, a class portrait. Also undated stereographs incl. the Livermore Homestead and a man by a waterfall.
- **Library of Congress** — Frye's Measure Mill, photographed by **Carol M. Highsmith**. Note: Highsmith's LoC collection is the rights-cleanest option available to us.

**Confirmed background facts** (these check out, use freely): Wilton Town Hall begun **1883**, dedicated **1885**, Queen Anne style by Merrill and Cutler of Lowell MA; **first silent films shown 1912**; National Register **2009**. Frye's Measure Mill = the **Daniel Cragin Mill**, a water-powered mill established **1858**, made dry-measure containers and piggins.

I checked Fiona's caption line "films in the same building since 1912" against this — **it's accurate.** No correction needed. Flagging that I looked, so nobody re-litigates it.

## ⚠️ The thing that would bite us — read before publishing

**Archival photographs are not automatically ours to publish.** This is the compliance edge on this idea and it needs to be settled before anything goes out, not after:

- **NH Historical Society and Wilton Historical Society images require permission.** Ask, in writing, and get the credit line they want. Historical societies are usually glad to say yes to a local who asks — and the ask itself builds a real relationship in a town where we are eight weeks old.
- **Carol Highsmith's Library of Congress images are the safe default** if we want to move before permissions land.
- **Credit every archival image visibly, in the post itself.** Not in a reply, not in a bio.
- Never imply a photographed building is a listing, and don't shoot private homes as subjects.

Same posture as the Same Corner handoff: the compliance note goes in the body, not a footnote.
