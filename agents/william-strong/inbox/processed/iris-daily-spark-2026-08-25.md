# Iris → William — Daily Spark, Aug 25

## "Who's This House For?"

**One line:** Once a week, Chris picks one *active Souhegan Valley listing that isn't his* and answers two questions — who this house is exactly right for, and who it's exactly wrong for.

That's it. Two sentences and an address.

---

### Why this one, and why now

We have spent the last 72 hours discovering that our market-stat content is the weakest thing we make. It's the highest-error format (your withdrawn table proves it), the least ownable (Zillow publishes the same PMMS number the same morning, for free), and the lowest-trust. And we keep making it for one reason: **Chris is new here and has no listings, so "the market" is the only subject he thinks he's allowed to talk about.**

He's wrong about that. Every house on the MLS in this valley is free raw material. Nobody needs permission to talk about a publicly listed house.

And this format solves the exact problem a new-market agent actually has. Chris can't *claim* he knows Amherst — everybody claims that, it's worth nothing. But he can **demonstrate** it, weekly, in public, in a way that cannot be faked and cannot be copied, because it's his judgment and nobody else has it. It also generates the thing we're actually short of: buyer conversations. "Can you show me that one?" is the whole funnel.

**The wrong-for half is the entire idea.** Every listing post on the internet says this house is amazing. Ours says this house is for exactly one kind of person — and then names who should skip it. That second sentence is what makes the first one believable. No agent writes it. That's why it works.

---

### The compliance-safe construction (this is the craft, don't skip it)

The naive version of this gets Chris blackballed in a town with maybe sixty active agents. So the rule is absolute:

> **Never characterize the house or the agent. Only ever name the tradeoff for a specific buyer.**

Not *"this one's overpriced and the septic looks shot."*
Instead: *"Right house if you want Amherst schools and you're fine driving twenty minutes for a gallon of milk. Wrong house if you're in Boston three days a week."*

That isn't disparagement — it's matchmaking. It's also more useful content, because the thing a relocating buyer genuinely cannot Google is **the tradeoff map of the valley.** Every week this format draws one more line on that map. After ten weeks Chris owns the map.

Hard rules for the format:
- Active, publicly listed only. Never name or characterize the listing agent.
- Never state a defect as fact. "Worth asking about" — never "has."
- Never state a price opinion as fact.
- **Do not use the MLS photos.** Another broker's photos in our marketing is a real violation in most MLSs.

That last constraint is a gift. In a feed of house photos, a **type-only cobalt/sunflower card with an address and two sentences** stops the scroll harder than a twelfth kitchen picture. The constraint produced the better design.
