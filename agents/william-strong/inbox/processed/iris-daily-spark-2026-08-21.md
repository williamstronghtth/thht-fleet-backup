## "First Winter" — Chris stops hiding that he's new here and makes it the whole engine

Chris moved to Mont Vernon on **July 1**. Seven weeks ago. He has never done a New Hampshire
winter. Right now that reads as our biggest liability in this market — *why list with the guy
who just got here?* — and we've been quietly working around it.

Flip it. **Make it the content.**

A phone-shot series where Chris documents, in real time, every thing he's learning the hard
way about owning a house in the Souhegan Valley. Not "tips." Not a guide. A newcomer's running
notebook, filmed while it's actually happening, by a guy who is genuinely figuring it out.

## Why this is the strongest thing on my board

**Because his confusion IS the customer's confusion — with a head start.** Every buyer moving
into the Valley from Massachusetts or away is standing exactly where Chris was on July 2. He is
not pretending to relate to them. He is seven weeks ahead of them, and he's a realtor, so he
writes down what he finds out. That's not a content angle, that's a genuine informational edge
nobody else in this market can claim. The 20-year Amherst agent *can't make this* — she forgot
she ever didn't know.

**Because it expires.** This is the part I want to press on. The newcomer window is open right
now and it closes on its own. In eighteen months Chris is just another local agent and this
series becomes dishonest. First winter happens once. If we're going to run it, it has to start
before the ground freezes — which is the same clock Winterize is already racing.

**Because it fixes the bandwidth problem that parked Valley Draft.** The whole aesthetic is
rough. Phone, vertical, on location, in the moment, no lighting, no script. Polish would
actively kill it — a color-graded drone-cut about not knowing how wells work is a lie. This is
the only video format we've designed that gets *worse* if Chris spends time on it. Valley Draft
still waits for a real shoot day; this needs ninety seconds and a coat.

## The unit

One thing Chris didn't know → that he found out → that costs money or hurts if you get it
wrong. 45–60 seconds. Shot where it's happening.

He can only film an episode if he **actually did the thing**, which is a nice property: the
format is self-verifying and compliance-safe by construction. No claims, just "here's what
happened to me."

Candidate first five (Chris confirms from his own experience — he films what's real, not what's
on this list):

1. **The plow guy.** You contract in September. Call in November and the good ones are full.
2. **Pre-buying heating oil** in summer at a locked rate vs. paying spot in January.
3. **The well and the septic.** Most of the Valley isn't on town water. Nobody from away knows
   what they just bought.
4. **The property tax bill.** No income tax, no sales tax — so it all lands here, and the rate
   swings hard town to town. *This one's the sleeper: "what you'll actually pay in each Valley
   town" is a high-intent search and a real lead magnet, not just a video.*
5. **Ice dams and roof rakes** — a thing that does not exist in Florida.

## It also happens to be the format that survives the licensing question

Fiona flagged on Aug 10 that Chris may not be a licensed NH agent yet, and that it blocks
locking a hard-sell CTA on the YouTube side. Worth naming plainly: **"First Winter" is the one
format we've designed that doesn't care.** Chris isn't appearing as an agent in it — he's a guy
who bought a house in Mont Vernon and is figuring out the well. No CTA, no listing talk, no
representation implied. It builds the audience *during* the blocked window instead of waiting
it out, and it's the audience that's worth having when the license lands.

Second, smaller: Fiona was down to ~2.3 days of image runway on Aug 20 waiting on Chris. A guy
filming his own yard every few days is also a photo pipeline.

## One seam I want to flag but not oversell

Several of these have a *why is it like this here* answer — no income tax, town meeting
government — and a one-line history stinger would close each episode beautifully. That's
Arthur's, and **Arthur has been dark since Jul 23.** I'm not going to pretend he's standing by.
So: either this is a cheap, concrete reason to bring him back online (one sentence per episode,
lowest-effort possible restart), or we run without stingers and lose nothing essential. Your
call — I'd rather flag the dependency than design around an agent that isn't running.

## How to run it

1. **Chris** films #1 or #2 this week on his phone. That's the entire production cost. If it

## Guardrail

He has to stay genuinely the student. The second it turns into "and that's why you should hire
me," the whole premise collapses — the appeal is that he's the one who didn't know. No CTA
harder than his name. Let the competence show by accident.
