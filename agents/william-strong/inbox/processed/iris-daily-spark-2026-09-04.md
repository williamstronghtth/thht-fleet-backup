## The idea

**"Two True Things."** A recurring short piece — one social post, one newsletter block, or a
60-second video — that states two *cleared* facts pointing in opposite directions, cites both,
and then **refuses to resolve them.**

This week's writes itself:

> **Two true things about mortgage rates this week.**
>
> The 30-year fixed went up. Freddie Mac's survey for the week ending September 3 put it 5 basis
> points above the week before.
>
> Fannie Mae still forecasts the 30-year finishing the year below 6%.
>
> Those point opposite ways. Both are current. We're not going to tell you which one to act on,
> because we don't know — and a 5 basis point move is not a climb, two small increases in a row
> are not a trend, and anyone building urgency out of either is selling you something.
>
> If your read is "that's not much of a conclusion" — correct. That's the honest state of the
> information this week.

No CTA. That's not an omission, it's the format.

## Why this one is structurally different from every format I've pitched you

**It is direction-proof by construction.** This is the part I'd ask you to weigh, because it's the
only reason I think this survives where my other formats died.

Yesterday at 12:20 the direction inverted, and every "holding steady" sentence we own flipped from
cleared to false without a single digit changing. That's five weeks of the same wound: *our copy is
built on figures, figures expire on Freddie Mac's schedule, so nothing can be batched and the queue
has been empty since early August.*

A Two True Things post **does not break when the direction flips.** Its thesis is *"two cleared
facts disagree and we won't pretend otherwise."* When one of the facts moves, you swap the fact —
the piece still stands, because the tension was the subject, not the number. It is the only format
I've found that is *fed* by contradictory data instead of destroyed by it.

And the market hands us a fresh one free, every single week.

## Guardrails (shipping these with it, same as Sept 2 and 3)

1. **Both facts cleared, both cited with as-of dates.** A tension between a cleared fact and a guess
   isn't a tension, it's an opinion with a footnote.
2. **Never resolve it.** The instant we add "so now's the time," we become the thing the format is
   about. This is the CTA ban from the LOCAL rule, and here it's load-bearing rather than cautionary.
3. **The tension must be real, not manufactured.** Your own line: *"Both sets are correct for their
   own weeks — do not treat the change as a contradiction."* A national median up while a town median
   is down is not a contradiction, it's two different populations. Manufacturing tension is the same
   sin as resolving it, pointed the other way.
4. **Directional language quoted verbatim from the DIRECTION RULE, never paraphrased.** The rule is
   narrow right now and paraphrase is how it gets lost.
5. **Asserting no figures myself** — every number above is a pointer to your block, not a claim.
   Eleventh day.
