## The Spark: **"The First Ten Days"**

### What I found

I read all nine just-sold write-ups sitting in `fiona-murphy/workspace/drafts/`. Every one is a real, closed, public-record sale from this year in our market. I tabled them by days on market against list price:

| Property | Town | DOM | vs. list |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 Founder's Way | Amherst | 0 | cash, $6,363,700 |
| 14 Boylston Terrace | Amherst | 0 | **+$45,000** |
| 15 Schwinn Drive | Nashua | 2 | **+$20,000** |
| 11 Pine Top Road | Amherst | 6 | **+$80,000** |
| 6 Klara Drive | Merrimack | 6 | **+$90,000** |
| 150 Greenville Road | Mason | 10 | **+$75,000** |
| — | — | — | — |
| 26 Snow Lane | Hollis | 20 | **−$75,000** |
| 95 Wright Road | Hollis | 68 | exactly list |
| 77 Broad Street | Hollis | 175 | **−$150,000** from original |

**There is nothing in the middle.** Not one house took 30 or 45 days. Everything either cleared inside ten days at or above ask, or crossed twenty days and finished at or below it. The market splits in the gap between day 10 and day 20, and after that a house is negotiating from behind.

### Why this is worth building content on

**It's ownable.** This has been my longest-running complaint: market-stat posts are our weakest format because Zillow gives the same number away free. Zillow publishes DOM. Nobody publishes the *shape* of it. Same public data, arranged so it says something no portal says. That's the difference between reposting a stat and having a read.

**It's a credential Chris can actually earn in eight weeks.** He can't say "I've sold forty homes here." He can say "I read every closed sale in this valley and here's the pattern." A new agent's honest advantage is attention, not tenure — this is the format that shows attention.

**It unblocks the thing that's been stuck.** "Who's This House For?" has sat blocked five days waiting on one active Souhegan listing. This needs no listing, no MLS photos, no shoot day. The raw material is already written up in Fiona's folder.

**It's the highest-value conversation a listing agent gets to have.** Every seller asks "what should we list at?" This gives Chris arithmetic to answer with instead of opinion — and it's genuinely useful to them, which is the whole point.

### The cross-pollination (why it'll land with Chris)

This is closing-line value, and Chris already thinks in it. Nolan and Elliot would recognize the shape on sight: a house that clears in six days at $80K over never had a real market price — the posted number was *below* where the market already was. The 175-day house was posted above it and had to walk down to meet it. The list price is the opening line. The first ten days are the market moving it. Everything after is the closing line.

That's not decoration — it's structurally the same mechanism, and it gives Chris a way to explain pricing to a seller in language he's fluent in.

### The second beat (this is the part that gets shared)

The fast bucket has two different species inside it, and the difference matters to a buyer:

- **Genuinely competitive** — Pine Top, Klara, Schwinn: over ask, two agents, real bidding.
- **Never really on the market** — Founder's Way (listed and closed the same day, all cash) and Boylston Terrace (0 DOM, same agent both sides). Those weren't fast. Those were *done before the listing was public.*

For a buyer new to the Souhegan Valley, that's the single most useful sentence anyone can say to them: **some houses here are spoken for before they ever appear on your app.** And it points straight at the action we actually want — a buyer conversation — without a word of urgency.

## Ready-to-run copy (first post)

> I pulled every closed sale I've written up in the Souhegan Valley this year. Nine houses. Amherst, Hollis, Nashua, Merrimack, Mason.
>
> Six of them sold in ten days or less. Every one of those went at or above asking.
>
> Three took twenty days or more. Every one of those went at or below.
>
> There was nothing in between. Not one house took a month and a half and then sold for what it asked.
>
> Nine houses isn't a law. It's nine houses. But if that holds, the list price isn't a starting point in this market — it's a test, and the market grades it in about a week and a half.
>
> I'm keeping the list. I'll tell you if it stops being true.

Ninety-eight words. No urgency, no "act now," no adjectives doing work the numbers already do. The last two lines are the whole thing — the admission that nine is a small sample is what makes it credible, and the promise to report back if it breaks is a reason to follow.

That's the same peer register that fixed Jack's Letter 01: assembling the list out loud, not dispensing expertise.

## How to run it

- **Format:** type-only card, cobalt/sunflower, the nine rows as a simple table. No photo. This is one of the rare pieces that's stronger unillustrated — and it sidesteps the generic-image problem entirely.
- **Cadence:** monthly, not weekly. The number of closed sales only grows so fast, and a monthly "here's what the list says now" is a franchise. Weekly would force it.
- **Upgrade path:** it's a two-minute video with zero B-roll — Chris on camera with the table on screen. Also a natural chapter in any town video.
- **Compliance, and this is the kill condition:** describe the *number's* behavior, never anyone's competence. Never "they overpriced it." The instant this reads as grading other agents it's dead, and deservedly. All nine are other brokerages' transactions; that's fine because it's public record and we're reporting, not claiming.
