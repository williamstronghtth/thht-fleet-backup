## ✨ The Spark: give Mont Vernon a thesis. Wilton got one; the town Chris actually lives in got a brochure.

### What I noticed

Put our last two town videos side by side.

**Wilton (Aug 28)** — *"The Town Time Kept."* A real point of view. A section on who the town is **not** for. A pinned comment. A thesis a stranger can repeat.

**Mont Vernon (Aug 3)** — *"A Complete Guide to This **Hidden** Southern New Hampshire Town,"* plus this line in the description:

> "the kind of place most people twenty miles off **have never heard of**"

Two problems, and the second one is the interesting one.

1. "Hidden" is the deadest word in real estate.
2. **It's factually backwards.**

### The material we already had and didn't use

Fiona researched Mont Vernon's history on **July 20** — two weeks *before* the video shipped. `fiona-murphy/workspace/memory/2026-07-20.md:105` has the Kittredge story sitting right there. It never made the description. **We found the best fact in the town and published the cliché instead.** That's a research-to-publish handoff gap, not a writing problem — and it's the cheapest kind to fix.

Here's what's verified (Wikipedia, cited; corroborated by the town's own About page at montvernonnh.us/1199/About):

- Late 1890s, Mont Vernon was a **tourist town** — **five large summer hotels**, including the **Grand Hotel on top of Grand Hill**, plus boarding houses. Boston emptied itself up here every summer.
- **George Kittredge, a local hotel owner, talked the U.S. Post Office into adding the "u"** — spelling it *Mount* Vernon — in the late 1800s, apparently to boost business. It was reverted after he died. The town's own site says the argument ran until the 1920s.
- The automobile (people could now reach the White Mountains) and then the Depression killed the hotels. Every one was **demolished or burned by World War II**.
- And the number that carries the whole thing — census population:

| 1800 | 1870 | **1930** | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 680 | 601 | **302** | 2,584 |

**Mont Vernon fell to 302 people.**

So: not a town nobody heard of. A town that was *famous*, whose hotelier rebranded it to get more Boston money, that emptied out to 302 people when the cars came — and that is now eight times that size because Boston-area people started driving north again.

We published "nobody's heard of it" about the one town in the valley that was a destination.

### The idea

**Retitle and rewrite the Mont Vernon video around one thesis: *"The Town That Emptied Out."***

And make it the sibling of Wilton on purpose:

> **Wilton — "The Town Time Kept."** Nothing changed.
> **Mont Vernon — "The Town That Emptied Out."** Everything did.

Same valley, opposite fates, in dialogue. **That's the thing I actually want you to take from this memo:** the town series needs *a thesis per town, and the theses should argue with each other.* That's what turns a playlist into a channel. New Boston is Sept 8 — it now has a bar to clear and a pattern to follow, before it gets written rather than after.

The cold open writes itself, and every word of it is checkable:

> "Five hotels stood in this town. Boston came up here every summer. Then the cars got better, the Depression hit, and by 1930 there were three hundred and two people left. I live here now. So do about twenty-five hundred other people."

No cliché. No "hidden." Nothing a Fair Housing gate would touch — it describes a town and its census, never the people in it.
