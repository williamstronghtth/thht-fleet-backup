## The idea: **The Winterize Letter**

Don't ask for the listing. Send the thing they actually need.

The out-of-state fiduciary's real problem isn't *should I sell.* It's *there is a
house full of my mother's things in a state I don't live in and I don't know who to
call.* That's a logistics problem, not a real-estate problem — and it has a hard
deadline they've never once thought about, because they live in Phoenix.

**An empty house in Hillsborough County has to be winterized before the first hard
freeze — usually early-to-mid October.** Nobody in Arizona knows that. Nobody in
Georgia knows that a standard homeowner's policy can stop covering a house after
30–60 days vacant. That single fact, offered with nothing attached to it, does more
to prove Chris is the local person than any market stat ever will.

**Opening line — this is the whole pitch:**

> *If the house on Elm Street is going to sit empty through November, someone needs
> to shut the water off before the first hard freeze. I'm not writing about selling
> it. I'm writing because I've seen what a burst pipe does to an empty house in
> February, and I'd rather that not be part of your year.*

No ask. Chris's name and number at the bottom. That's the letter.

## Why it wins

- **It's a gift, not a pitch.** The only envelope in the stack that isn't asking for
  something. It gets opened, and more importantly, it gets *kept* — stuck to a fridge
  2,000 miles away.
- **It converts on delay, which is the right timeline anyway.** Estates take months.
  When they finally sell, there is exactly one name they know in New Hampshire.
- **It makes the 14-day probate hold make sense.** The hold exists so we're not
  vulturing a fresh death. A caretaking letter with zero ask is precisely the message
  that's *appropriate* on day 14 — the rule stops being a brake and becomes the brand.
- **Un-copyable.** A wholesaler in Manchester cannot send this. He doesn't have the
  vendor list and he isn't licensed to care.
- **It turns Ryan's OOS flag into the creative wedge.** He called out-of-state the
  strongest signal in the track. He's right — but it's not just a motivation signal,
  it's a *knowledge gap* signal. That gap is the opening.
