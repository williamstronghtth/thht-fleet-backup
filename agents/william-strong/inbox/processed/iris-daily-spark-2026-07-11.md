## The Idea: "The One Question Email"

Swap one email in Jack's NH prospecting drip for a single sentence and one genuine question. No pitch. No credentials. No value proposition. No CTA button.

**Subject line:** `quick question`

**Body:**
> *"Hey [First Name] — quick question: if you were choosing between Amherst and Milford for your next move, what would make you pick one over the other?"*

That's the entire email.

---

**Why this works:**

Most prospecting emails fail because they sell before they listen. Everyone who's been browsing Souhegan Valley listings is already thinking about the area — they have opinions. This email just asks for one. The "quick question" framing signals: *this costs you 15 seconds, not 10 minutes.* People answer that.

The replies are gold. Anyone who writes back has self-identified as warm. They're telling Jack (and Chris) exactly what they care about — commute, schools, vibe, price — which feeds directly into follow-up calls, listing copy, and YouTube content. You can't buy that intel.

And it's different. Every other agent in the Souhegan Valley is sending "Hi, I'm [name], I've sold 47 homes in NH, call me to chat." Jack sends a question. That alone gets it opened.

**The cross-pollination:** This is lifted from B2B demand-gen research — single-question cold emails consistently outperform multi-paragraph pitches by 3–5x on response rate. Nobody applies it to real estate. It's a well-proven weapon hiding in plain sight.

---

**How to run it:**

1. Jack inserts this as email #3 or #4 in the NH prospecting sequence — after the warm intro but before the hard pitch.
2. Any reply triggers a 24-hour follow-up call from Chris: *"Thanks for writing back — you made me curious, what's drawing you to that area?"*
3. Over time, the reply patterns tell Chris which towns have the most buyer appetite and why — sharpens targeting for direct mail, Google Ads, and YouTube topics.

**One variation worth testing:** swap the Amherst/Milford geography for whatever two towns Jack is currently most active in.
