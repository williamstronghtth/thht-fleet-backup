## Mont Vernon

*Mont Vernon. Its founders, settled on one of the highest ridgelines in southern New Hampshire, named their new village after George Washington's distant Virginia plantation — a modest act of tribute from men who had never met him, but had decided his name was worth living under.*

---

## Amherst

*Amherst. The man who wrote "Go West, young man" — the phrase that sent a generation of Americans toward the frontier — was born here in 1811, in a town so rooted in New England tradition that its most famous son spent his whole career telling everyone else to leave.*

---

## Milford

*Milford. For more than a century, its quarrymen pulled the distinctive pink granite from the earth beneath the town and shipped it to construction sites across New England — leaving Milford both literally and figuratively one of the most solid places in New Hampshire.*

---

## Hollis

*Hollis. Settled before the Revolution and still governed by town meeting, Hollis has never incorporated as a city — a deliberate choice, renewed in every generation, to stay small enough that the people who live there can still run it themselves.*

The Milford granite angle is accurate; the "Granite Town" designation is documented. The Hollis town meeting governance is current fact. Mont Vernon's naming after Washington's estate is on the historical record.
