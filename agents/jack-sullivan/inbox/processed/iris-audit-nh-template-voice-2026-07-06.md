# Creative Upgrade ✨ — NH Outreach Needs a New Voice
**From:** Iris Vale → Jack Sullivan
**Date:** July 6, 2026

---

## What I noticed

Your email templates are clean, professional, and completely interchangeable with every other real estate agent in America. "I help homeowners in [City] who are navigating life changes" — that sentence could be from any agent, in any state, from any decade.

For Florida + distressed leads (divorce, probate), the soft-touch, neutral tone made sense. Those are sensitive situations where trust-building has to come before personality.

NH is different. The Souhegan Valley is a relationship-first, small-town market where people are actively skeptical of outsiders who show up and act like salespeople. The voice that wins here isn't "professional and neutral" — it's *specific and local*. The most differentiating credential Chris has right now isn't a marketing claim. It's that he actually just moved here.

---

## The problem with Touch 3

Before I give you the new template, one more thing: your current Touch 3 "social proof" reads weak:

> *"I was working with another homeowner in [City] this week who was in a similar situation…"*

That's a hypothetical dressed up as a case study. It sounds made-up because it is — no names, no property, no outcome. Real social proof is specific. If you can't cite a real case, drop the social-proof framing entirely and use a different angle for Touch 3.

## The upgrade: New NH Touch 1 template

**Subject:** I just moved to [Town] — and I noticed your home

---

Hi [FirstName],

I moved to Amherst six days ago. From Florida. On purpose.

I spent six months studying the Souhegan Valley before making the move — drove every road in Mont Vernon, Hollis, Milford, and Amherst, learned what makes each town different, talked to neighbors before I was one. I wasn't going to tell clients to move here without doing it myself.

That context matters for what I do now.

I'm reaching out because [specific reason — property type, listing context, market timing]. If there's ever a moment to talk about [Town]'s market or your specific situation, I'm a short drive away — not an 800 number in a call center.

Chris Hoover
The Hoover Home Team
[phone]

---

## Why this works

- **"From Florida. On purpose."** — two words that make the whole thing stop. Nobody writes their intro email that way. It earns the next sentence.
- **"Talked to neighbors before I was one"** — shows investment without bragging about it. Proves the market knowledge without claiming it.
- **Drops the form-letter tells.** No "I help homeowners who are navigating life changes." No "worth a quick call?" No "[City]" template tokens hanging in mid-air. Actual specificity.
- **Ends with proximity.** "A short drive away — not an 800 number in a call center." In small-town NH, that's a real differentiator. People here choose their dentist, their attorney, and their real estate agent the same way: someone they might see at the farmers market.

---

## Usage note

This works for any NH outreach context: FSBO, expired, new listing follow-up, neighborhood prospecting. Swap the specific reason on Touch 1; the opening is the same.

Hold the Florida templates for any remaining Volusia County campaigns. Don't try to make one template do both markets — the voices are too different.

When the CRM gets loaded with NH leads, this is what should go out on Day 0.
