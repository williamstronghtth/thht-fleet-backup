# Cross-Pollination

I'm the only agent who sees all of Chris's worlds at once. Every other agent reads inside its own lane. That means the obvious-to-me move — "the way Nolan frames a betting edge is exactly how Jack should frame a listing" — is invisible to everyone else. This skill turns that vantage into a repeatable engine.

The principle: **creativity is mostly transfer.** A "new" idea is usually an old idea from somewhere else, wearing different clothes. So instead of straining to invent from nothing, I go shopping across domains and bring back what fits.

## The domains I draw from

Chris's worlds, each with patterns worth stealing:

- **Sports betting / prediction markets** (Nolan, Calvin, Elliot, Oliver): edges, confidence buckets, expected value, contrarian plays, "the market is wrong about X," line movement, conviction levels.
- **Fantasy sports** (Eno; Chris's IDP football & fantasy baseball): sleepers, busts, projections, draft strategy, the underrated mid-round value pick, tiers.
- **History storytelling** (Arthur): the hook-tension-payoff beat, "on this day," forgotten stories, the surprising detail that recontextualizes everything.
- **Soccer tactics** (Chris's interest): formations, pressing, creating space, the overlap, exploiting a mismatch, set-piece design.
- **Geography / geolocation games** (KoalaKlub GeoSports): reading subtle clues, deduction, "where in the world," local detail as identity.
- **Nutrition** (Willow): habit stacking, small consistent reps, the 80/20, sustainable cadence over crash effort.
- **Real estate** (Jack, the core): listings, neighborhoods, the buyer's journey, market updates, trust-building.
- **Roofing** (Derek): urgency, trust, local proof, seasonal timing, the high-consideration purchase.
- **Music** (Mumford & Sons, Irish folk): build-and-release dynamics, the quiet-then-loud, storytelling in verse, communal feeling.

## The process

1. **Name the stuck thing.** What needs a fresh angle? (e.g. "market-update videos feel like every other agent's.")
2. **Pick a far-away domain.** The further from real estate, the fresher the result. Reach for betting, soccer, history, or fantasy before reaching for "other real estate ideas."
3. **Extract the pattern, not the surface.** Don't copy the *thing* — copy the *mechanism*. From betting, the surface is "odds"; the mechanism is "rank your confidence and tell people where you have conviction."
4. **Transplant and translate.** Apply the mechanism to the stuck thing, then dress it in the target domain's language.
5. **Sanity-check fit.** Does it actually serve the goal, or is it clever-for-clever's-sake? Keep it only if it makes the work *better*, not just *weirder*.

## Worked examples (the move in action)

**Betting → listings.** Mechanism: confidence buckets / "where the market is wrong." → Jack's listing copy leads with *the one thing this house has that comparable listings don't* — its "edge" — stated as plainly as a betting writeup states a play. Not "charming home"; "the only house on the street with a detached studio."

**History → social.** Mechanism: Arthur's hook-tension-payoff and "on this day." → Fiona runs an "On This Street" series: the surprising history or hidden detail of a Souhegan Valley town, opening with a hook, building a small mystery, paying it off. Hyperlocal, un-copyable, and it makes THHT *the* local authority.

**Fantasy baseball → market reports.** Mechanism: sleepers and tiers. → A "Souhegan Valley Sleepers" piece: the underrated towns/neighborhoods buyers are overlooking and why they're about to pop. Borrows the thrill of spotting value early.

**Nutrition → drip campaigns.** Mechanism: habit stacking, small consistent reps over crash effort. → Jack's outreach cadence designed like a habit plan: small, consistent, value-first touches that compound, instead of one big salesy blast.

**Soccer → video structure.** Mechanism: build-up play, creating space before the shot. → Video scripts that "create space" early (set up curiosity, widen the field) before the "shot" (the call to action), instead of leading with the ask.

**Geolocation games → community pages.** Mechanism: tiny local clues signal identity. → The `thht-communities` pages lean into hyper-specific local tells — the diner everyone means when they say "the diner," the shortcut locals take — the details that prove you actually *know* the town.

## When to use it

Reach for cross-pollination during any brainstorm, but *especially* when an idea feels like something you've seen before. If the first batch of ideas all smell like real-estate ideas, that's the signal to leave the neighborhood and go borrow from a sport, a betting model, or a history story. Pair this with `idea-generation` (it's a divergence technique) and feed the result into `hook-and-headline` or `storytelling-frameworks` to finish it.
