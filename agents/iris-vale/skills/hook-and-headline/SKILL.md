# Hook & Headline

The first line does 80% of the work. A great video with a weak first three seconds is an unwatched video. A perfect email with a dead subject line is an unopened email. This skill is about winning that first moment across every format the team uses.

## The one rule under all the others

**Open with the most interesting thing, not the setup.** The instinct is to warm up — context, throat-clearing, "Hi everyone, today I want to talk about…" Cut all of it. Start in the middle of the most surprising, useful, or emotional moment. Earn the next line, then the next.

## Hook formulas that work

Pick by format and goal; never force one:

- **The curiosity gap.** Open a loop the audience needs closed. "This house sat unsold for 90 days. One $200 change fixed it." They have to know what.
- **The specific number.** Specificity reads as true and concrete. "3 Amherst neighborhoods buyers keep ignoring" beats "great neighborhoods in Amherst."
- **The contrarian take.** Challenge a belief. "Everyone tells you to price high and negotiate down. Here's why that's costing you." (Borrow the betting-agent instinct: *the market is wrong about X.*)
- **The stakes / cost.** Name what's at risk. "The mistake that's quietly adding $8,000 to your roof replacement."
- **In medias res.** Drop them into a moment already in motion. "We almost lost this deal at the inspection."
- **The pattern interrupt.** Say the thing people don't expect a real estate agent to say. Disarms the ad-radar.
- **The direct promise.** When the audience is already motivated, just deliver the value fast: "Move to the Souhegan Valley? Here's the 5-minute version of everything you need to know."

## Format-specific craft

**Video first 3 seconds (Fiona/William).** Visual + verbal hook together. The frame on screen should be as arresting as the first sentence. No logos, no slow intros, no "what's up guys." The viewer decides to stay before you've finished your first breath.

**YouTube title + thumbnail (Fiona).** Title and thumbnail are *one unit* — they shouldn't repeat each other, they should combine. Thumbnail shows the emotion or the surprising image; title supplies the curiosity gap the thumbnail opened. Title under ~60 characters so it's not cut off. Lead with the value or the intrigue, not the topic label.

**Email subject lines (Jack).** Short (aim 4–7 words), specific, and curiosity-or-value forward. Write the email first, then pull the single most interesting thing in it up into the subject. Never bait-and-switch — the open has to be honored by the body or you burn trust. Avoid spammy energy (ALL CAPS, "FREE!!", excessive punctuation). For NH cold outreach especially: sound like a neighbor, not a marketer.

**Direct mail first line (Jack / Twriter campaigns).** A handwritten piece already has the open — they're holding it. So the *first sentence* is the hook. Make it feel personal and human in the first seven words. Never start with "As a local real estate agent…"

**Roofing ads (Derek).** Different audience, different lever: trust and urgency over delight. Lead with the problem they already feel or the cost of waiting. Local proof.

**History posts (Arthur).** The hook is the surprising detail or the "wait, that happened *here / today*?" Open on the strangest true fact, not the date.

## Generate, then choose

Never write one headline. Write 8–10 fast (use `idea-generation`), then pick the strongest. Run each survivor through:
- **The skim test:** would this stop *me* mid-scroll?
- **The specificity test:** could a competitor run this exact line? If yes, make it more particular to this house/town/person/data.
- **The honesty test:** does the rest of the content actually deliver what the hook promised?

## Deliver

Hand back a short ranked shortlist (top 3, plus a couple of alternates), with the boldest defensible option first. For a YouTube ask, deliver title + matching thumbnail concept as a pair. Keep voice matched to the agent and the market (FL coastal/established vs. NH small-town/relationship-first).
