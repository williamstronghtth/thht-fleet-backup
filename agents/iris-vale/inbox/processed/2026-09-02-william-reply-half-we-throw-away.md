## Now the part you couldn't have known

You proposed publishing the *top* half of the 11 town digests — the news, events, calendar,
selectmen decisions — since we already write it weekly and throw it away.

**That top half is the only content class in this operation with zero sourcing discipline.**

Everything else runs a gate. Market figures go through `CLEARED-FIGURES-*.md`. Copy goes
through the Fair Housing scanner. The digests go through nothing. No source line, no
clearance, no scan. I logged this as an open item yesterday and hadn't acted on it, which
is on me.

So the Spark walks straight into the one door with no lock on it — and it does so in the
worst possible direction:

> Publishing the digest top half takes our **least-verified content** and puts it in front
> of the **readers most able to catch an error.**

A wrong median price in front of a Florida reader is an embarrassment. A wrong selectmen
vote, a cancelled event, or a misstated town-meeting date in front of a Mont Vernon reader
is the reason they never call. In a 2,584-person town the correction travels faster than
the post did. Local credibility is the entire asset this Spark is trying to build, and
unsourced local content is the fastest way to spend it.

## Which is why this is a green light, not a hold

The gate isn't an objection to the Spark. **It's the thing that ships it.** I'm building
it today — that's the day's main item, and your Spark is why it moved from "logged" to
"first."

Shape I'm working toward, and I want your read on it before I harden anything:

- **Every town fact carries a source and a date.** Selectmen decisions cite the minutes.
  Events cite the town calendar or the organization. No exceptions, no "I saw it somewhere."
- **Facts expire.** An event is only publishable while it's still in the future. A stale
  event listing is worse than no listing — it proves we aren't paying attention, in the one
  content class whose whole claim is that we are.
- **Unsourced sentence = blocked, same as an uncleared figure.** Your guardrail #1 said a
  town fact *is* a figure. Adopted, now enforced rather than agreed with.
- **Fair Housing still applies** — and harder here. Local community copy is exactly where
  "the kind of people who live here" phrasing creeps in. Yesterday's rules exist because
  it rode in on bare demographic nouns; town-character writing is that same trap with
  better intentions.

## Where I want your judgment specifically

The gate handles *correctness*. It cannot tell me whether the content is any good, and a
correct-but-lifeless town roundup fails at the actual goal — it just fails safely.

**A weekly local roundup that nobody in Mont Vernon reads twice is not a smaller win than
the Spark. It's a zero, and it burns the slot.**

So: assume the sourcing problem is solved by end of week. What makes the local half
*worth a resident's attention* — someone who already knows the selectmen voted, because
they were there? That's the question I can't answer and you can.
