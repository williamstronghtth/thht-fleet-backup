**Subject:** The roofing blog is leaving the best July hook on the table

---

## What I spotted

Great call publishing the repair-vs-replacement post — that's a real content gap, and it's a high-intent keyword. But I read it and noticed something: it reads like it could have been published in January. Or May. Or next October.

Derek, your own rationale for the topic included "hurricane season = peak damage discovery." You saw the hook. You just didn't use it in the post.

In July in Volusia County, a homeowner's emotional state isn't "hmm, let me research my long-term roofing options." It's closer to: "The season starts in two weeks. My roof is twenty years old. I don't know if I'm okay. I don't want to be the person whose ceiling comes in during a storm."

That's anxiety, not curiosity. And anxiety converts differently than curiosity.

---

## The upgrade

The next post should be: **"72 Hours After the Storm: What Volusia County Homeowners Need to Do Before the Adjuster Arrives"**

This is the moment where leads actually call. Not when they're calmly deciding between repair and replacement — when the storm already happened and they're scared and don't know what to do next.

---

Then: what to photograph before the tarp goes up, what your insurance company expects to see, what an assignment of benefits clause actually means and why you shouldn't sign one, how to tell a storm chaser from a legitimate local contractor, and why calling Affordable Roofing before the adjuster visit matters.

That last point is where the call comes in. The post earns it.

---

## Why this converts better

"Roof repair vs. replacement" is what someone searches when they have time and no crisis. Volume: decent. Intent: medium.

"What to do after storm damages my roof" / "roof damage insurance claim Florida" — that's what someone searches at 11 PM the night after a storm while looking out their window. Volume: lower but spiky. Intent: calling-a-contractor-tomorrow high.

Volusia County is entering its highest-storm-probability window of the year. The post that's live when the first big storm hits this season will generate more calls than six months of repair-vs-replacement content combined.

---

## Timing

Write it now. Publish it this week. Every week it's live before a named storm is a week of search-index credit you've built.

The hurricane season content brief is: write for the person standing in their living room looking at water stains on the ceiling, not for the person reading a comparison article on a Tuesday afternoon.
