## The Spark: "The Scouting Report"

**One-line:** Evaluate each Souhegan Valley town like Eno evaluates a fantasy baseball player — surface stats vs. underlying quality, and why the market is sleeping on it.

**The concept:**

Fantasy baseball analysts (Eno's world) do something real estate agents almost never do: they separate *surface stats* from *underlying signals* to find undervalued assets before everyone else. The format is tight, structured, and flatters the viewer's intelligence.

Apply it to Chris's NH towns. Each episode is a 2-minute "Scouting Report" on one town:

> "Surface read on Milford: small mill town, not as prestigious as Amherst, median price $80k lower. Underlying read: the school district's performance gap with Amherst has narrowed by 40% in five years. There's a rail trail system that walk-score algorithms can't count. Three families from Massachusetts moved in last month within two streets of each other. The market is pricing 2021 Milford. You're buying 2026 Milford."

That last line — *you're buying 2026 Milford* — is the hook the data-literate relocation buyer actually wants to hear.

**Why it works:**
- Completely differentiated from any content any NH agent is making (they all do tours; nobody does analytical scouting)
- The format does the positioning work — you're analytical, trustworthy, and ahead of the market by definition
- Speaks directly to Chris's actual buyer: the professional relocating from MA/CT/NY who runs spreadsheets before they ever pick up a phone
- Infinitely repeatable — every Souhegan Valley town gets its own episode, each one an SEO asset
- Easy to shoot: Chris talks to camera with a few B-roll cuts; no complicated production

**The cross-pollination source:** Eno Sarris's daily fantasy baseball scouting methodology. The language of "what the surface says vs. what the underlying data reveals" is Eno's bread and butter — Chris just moves it from ERA- and xFIP to school district trends and price-per-square-foot trajectories.

**First episode candidate:** Milford, NH. Priced like a secret, close to Amherst quality, and few buyers are looking there yet. That's a scouting find.
