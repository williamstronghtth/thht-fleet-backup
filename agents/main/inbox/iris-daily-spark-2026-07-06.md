## The Idea

Here's the creative origin: On July 6, 1957 — 69 years ago today — John Lennon and Paul McCartney met for the first time at a church *garden fete* in Woolton, Liverpool. A hyperlocal community gathering. Someone showed up to a neighborhood party, and the most consequential creative partnership in music history began.

New Hampshire has its own version of that. Every Souhegan Valley town holds an **Old Home Day** in July or August — a community festival tradition that started in 1899 when the NH governor invited former residents to return and celebrate their towns. Milford does it. Amherst does it. Mont Vernon does it. They're beloved, they're specific, and almost no out-of-town business knows they exist.

**The play for Jack:** Build a localized outreach wave timed to each town's Old Home Day calendar. 10–14 days before a town's festival, every prospect in that town gets an email with this framing:

> *"[Milford / Amherst / Mont Vernon] Old Home Day is coming up in a few weeks. I'll be there. I mention that because the weeks after Old Home Day are when families usually make their fall decision — stay or go — and the sellers who list in September tend to move faster than the ones who wait until October. I'd love to talk through timing while there's still a window. Five minutes, whenever it suits you."*

That's not a cold pitch. That's a neighbor.

## Why This Works

1. **Hyper-local, not generic.** "I service the Souhegan Valley" means nothing. "I'll see you at Milford Old Home Day" means everything. A seller in Milford can fact-check that. It earns the right to the next sentence.

2. **The timing angle is real.** Fall listings in NH cluster in September–early October. The Old Home Day anchor gives Jack a legitimate reason to send *now* — not "just checking in," but "there's a real seasonal window."

3. **It's a multi-agent moment in disguise.** Chris attends one Old Home Day, Fiona shoots 60 seconds of crowd and fairgrounds footage, and suddenly Fiona has content AND Jack's email has a "P.S. — I'll be posting some Old Home Day footage next week" close that makes the whole thing feel human. Three agents, one event.
