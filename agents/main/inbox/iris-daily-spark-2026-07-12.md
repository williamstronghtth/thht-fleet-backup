## The Spark

**"On Location: [Town]"** — a weekly YouTube Short (60 seconds, vertical) where Chris records one market insight from somewhere *physically specific* in the Souhegan Valley.

Not a studio video. Not a polished sit-down. Chris, a real place, one data point.

## Why it's strong

**The location IS the credential.** Any agent in Boston can make a video about the Souhegan Valley. Nobody else is actually standing in it, talking from it, on a Saturday morning when the market is alive. The geography becomes proof that Chris is the local — because he keeps showing up in the local.

**It borrows from two other agents' DNA:**
- *From Nolan's model:* the framing of "what's not priced in yet" — the angle where you tell people the thing the market hasn't caught up to. That's the insight. Not "it's a great place to live" (everyone says that), but "here's the data point most buyers are sleeping on right now."
- *From Arthur:* the historical-present device — speaking from a specific place and moment makes the story feel immediate and true, not evergreen-bland.

**It's uncopyable.** An out-of-market competitor cannot fake 52 videos of Chris standing in 52 different Souhegan Valley locations. That library, one year from now, is an asset no one can replicate fast.

**It's low-production.** No script. No B-roll assembly. No editing beyond a title card. Chris shoots it on the DJI or his phone, hands it to Fiona for packaging. 20 minutes end-to-end.

## The format spec (Fiona can run with this)

- **Length:** 55–65 seconds
- **Opening:** drone or wide establishing shot of the location (5–8 sec), then cut to Chris handheld
- **Chris's structure:** Where I am → What I'm seeing in the market RIGHT NOW → One specific number or observation → What it means for a buyer or seller in this town
- **No CTA:** Resist the urge. The value IS the CTA. Let the watch history do the work.
- **Title card end:** "On Location: [Town] — [Month Year]" — simple, archivable, searchable
- **Post everywhere:** YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, Facebook — identical cut, no adjustment needed
