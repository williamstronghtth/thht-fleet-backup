## The Spark

Every real estate intro letter in New Hampshire says the same thing: *Hi, I'm Chris, I'm new to the area, I'd love to help you buy or sell.*

None of them say: *I drove your street last Tuesday.*

Here's the format for Jack to build the NH prospecting sequence around:

> **Opening line (handwritten via Twriter Machine):**  
> "I've been driving every road in [Town] since I moved here last month — and [Street Name] is one I keep coming back to."

One sentence. No agent pitch yet. Just: *I was specifically on your street, and I noticed.*

Then, two more lines maximum:
- One observation that's impossible to fake (something Chris actually sees on that street — the lot depth, the mature maples, the way it backs up to conservation land)
- One specific market fact: "Three homes within a half mile of yours sold in the last 90 days. I know exactly what that means for yours."

Close with his name, number, and one line: "No pitch, just the number — whenever you're curious."

That's the whole letter. Short enough to read before the recycling bin. Specific enough that it can't be mass-produced.

## Why this works

**It borrows Chris's real credential.** He *is* the new neighbor. He *has* been driving the streets. The letter doesn't perform local knowledge — it is local knowledge, because he actually went there. Every competitor fakes the local-expert angle. Chris is the only one who can back it up with the genuine just-moved-in energy he has *right now*, before it fades.

**Specificity is the filter.** Junk mail is generic. A letter that names your street, mentions something true about it, and includes a real data point does not look like junk mail. It looks like someone paid attention.

**The Twriter Machine makes it tactile.** It arrives as a physical, handwritten-looking letter in a world of printed postcards. That alone earns a second glance. The content earns the read.

**The timing window is closing.** Chris's "new to the area" positioning has a shelf life. The first-year-in-town energy — the genuine curiosity of someone who just discovered these streets — depreciates by the month. Jack should build this sequence now, while the posture is authentic.
