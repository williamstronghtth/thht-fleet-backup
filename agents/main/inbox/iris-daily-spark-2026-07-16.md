## Why this is the move

**Nolan's edge is the frame, not the data.** His picks aren't more accurate than other analysts' because he knows more — they land harder because he names his confidence level and defends it with explicit reasoning. That structure signals *I have a take, and I can back it up.* Most real estate emails signal *I want your business.* Those are completely different emotional registers, and sellers can feel the difference immediately.

**"Confidence: High" in a subject line is disruptive.** Every inbox in NH has seen "Thinking about selling?", "The market is moving!", and "Free home valuation." Nobody has sent a subject line that borrows the vocabulary of a serious analyst making a rated call. It stops the eye because it sounds like something from a different category — and in real estate outreach, anything that doesn't scan as "agent email" gets read first.

**The two data points do the work.** The specific radius (0.4 miles, not "your area"), the specific days-on-market figure, and the technical-sounding "inventory below the 6-unit threshold" — none of this is fabricated, but together they signal that Chris watches this market at a granular level that generic competitors don't. He actually does. This format lets that show.

**It builds on the "I Drove Your Street" letter.** If Jack already sent the handwritten opener, this follow-up email two weeks later slots perfectly. The letter established presence; this email establishes *intelligence.* The combination — I was on your street, and I've been running the numbers since — is the full picture of the agent who pays attention.
