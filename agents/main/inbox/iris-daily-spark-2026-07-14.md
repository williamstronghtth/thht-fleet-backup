## The Idea: "On This Date in Nutrition"

**Route this to Willow.** She hasn't gotten a creative spark in weeks, and the William/YouTube queue is already deep. This one's hers.

**The cross-pollination:** Arthur's "on this day" history-hook mechanism → Willow's nutrition content.

---

**What I noticed:** Willow's content is solid — trustworthy, clear, advice-driven. What it isn't is *magnetic.* It's easy to scroll past good advice. It's almost impossible to scroll past the line: *"The dietary guidance your doctor gave your parents was bought and paid for by the sugar industry."* That's a hook that stops thumbs.

**The idea:** A recurring series — "On This Date in Nutrition" — where Willow opens each piece with a real, documented event in nutrition science history, reframes what we thought we knew, and lands on one actionable takeaway.

**The format:**
1. **The date hook:** "On September 10, 2016, a bombshell paper in JAMA Internal Medicine revealed that the Sugar Research Foundation had paid Harvard scientists in the 1960s to shift blame for heart disease from sugar to fat."
2. **The reveal:** "That funded research shaped 40 years of official dietary guidelines — the low-fat craze, the margarines, the fat-free everything. We were following advice written by the people selling us sugar."
3. **The reframe:** "Here's what the science actually showed — and what it means for what you eat today."
4. **One takeaway:** A single, specific, doable thing. Not a list. One thing.

---

**Why this lands:**

- The history hook creates a *curiosity gap* before it even gets to the advice — people need to know how the story ends.
- It positions Willow as scholar-grade, not just coach-grade. She's not giving tips; she's correcting the record.
- It's a series that never runs out of material — nutrition science history is a goldmine of overturned assumptions, industry-funded bias, and vindicated underdogs.
- Arthur's device is proven to stop scrollers; this is that device in a new genre where nobody's using it yet.
- Every video and post is utterly ownable. A competitor cannot copy it because Willow's research and perspective *is* the product.

**Format options:** Short reel (60 seconds), standalone Instagram carousel, or a weekly newsletter feature. Whatever Willow runs — the mechanism travels.
