**Idea: The New Neighbor Letter — Chris's most powerful NH intro campaign, hiding in plain sight**

Here's the thing almost no agent ever does when they enter a new market: they introduce themselves as *a neighbor*, not a salesperson. Chris can't do this forever — but right now, in the weeks after his family moves to Amherst, he has a window that will never open again.

**The concept:** A single-page handwritten-style letter (Twriter Machine, obviously) sent to 200–300 homes across the Souhegan Valley — not as a mass mailer, but as a genuine moving-in introduction. The voice is personal and specific, not promotional. Something like:

> *"Hi — I'm Chris. My family just moved to Amherst from Port Orange, Florida, and we're still discovering what makes this town quietly extraordinary. We already love [one real, specific thing — the stone walls along Mack Hill Road, the way the common looks at 7am, whatever Chris genuinely notices first week]. I'm also a real estate agent, so if you ever think about buying or selling here, I'd love to earn a chance to help. I know what it feels like to transplant your whole life to a new place — and I don't take that lightly. — Chris"*

No bullet points. No stats. No "limited time." Just a human being, in a new town, telling the truth.

**Why this wins:**
Most direct mail screams *I want your listing.* This one whispers *I live here too.* That's a completely different emotional register — and in small-town NH, where relationships are the whole game, it lands in a category of one. People who move to Amherst and Mont Vernon chose those towns *on purpose.* They responded to character, history, community. A letter that speaks that language — without selling — becomes a refrigerator pin, not a recycling bin.

**The cross-pollination that makes it click:** In sports betting, the sharpest move is to act before the market adjusts to the information. Chris has a very short window where "I just moved here and I'm an agent" is a genuine story rather than a sales narrative. That window closes fast. This letter deploys that edge *now*, before it's gone.

**How to execute:** Route to Jack — this is a one-time variant on his direct mail sequence, not a drip. One letter, one moment, one authentic impression. Jack drafts the copy (Chris refines based on his actual first-week observations), runs it through the Twriter Machine, targets 200 homes across Amherst, Mont Vernon, and Milford. Chris's cell or email at the bottom, no CTA pressure. Timing: send within the first two weeks of the move.

**The detail that makes it irreplaceable:** Whatever Chris notices first — the thing that surprises him about Amherst — that goes in the letter. If he spots a 1790s stone wall on his morning run and can't stop thinking about it, that's the line. Specific > polished. A reader can feel the difference between a person and a template.
